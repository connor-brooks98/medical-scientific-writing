Sepsis remains a leading cause of death worldwide, representing a pivotal challenge in the evolving landscape of critical care. In recent years, there has been growing interest in the role of early vasopressor initiation. Studies have shown that early norepinephrine improves survival in septic shock. Furthermore, a growing body of evidence suggests that delayed vasopressor therapy may potentially be associated with worse outcomes in some patients.

In a retrospective analysis of 2,914 patients with septic shock at our institution, norepinephrine started within 1 hour of shock onset was associated with lower 28-day mortality (adjusted odds ratio, 0.78; 95% CI, 0.64–0.94). Early norepinephrine therefore prevents death in septic shock. To address this critical gap, we designed a comprehensive prospective study that will not only fill an important gap in the literature but also pave the way for improved outcomes.
