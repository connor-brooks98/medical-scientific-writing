# Response to Reviewers

**Reviewer 1, Comment 1:** The authors should report the number of patients lost to follow-up in each group.

**Response:** We sincerely thank the reviewer for this insightful and valuable comment, which has greatly helped us to improve the quality of our manuscript. Loss to follow-up is indeed an important consideration in any longitudinal study, as it can introduce attrition bias if it differs between groups. In our original submission, we reported overall loss to follow-up but did not break it down by group. Upon reflection, we agree that this information would be helpful to readers. Therefore, we have now added the number of patients lost to follow-up in each group to the CONSORT diagram (Figure 1) and to the Results (page 9, lines 182–184): 14 patients (4.1%) in the intervention group and 17 patients (5.0%) in the control group.

**Reviewer 1, Comment 2:** Please justify the choice of a 90-day primary endpoint rather than a 1-year endpoint.

**Response:** We thank the reviewer for this excellent and thought-provoking question. The choice of endpoint is a crucial aspect of trial design that requires careful consideration of multiple factors, including biological plausibility, clinical relevance, and feasibility. We carefully considered both 90-day and 1-year endpoints during the design phase. Most readmissions after heart failure hospitalization occur within the first 90 days, and the intervention was designed to act during the transition period. A 1-year endpoint would also have required a larger sample size that was not feasible within our funding. We have therefore kept the 90-day endpoint and added this rationale to the Methods (page 6, lines 121–125).

**Reviewer 2, Comment 1:** The discussion overstates the generalizability of the findings to all patients with heart failure.

**Response:** We are grateful to the reviewer for raising this important and valid point. Generalizability is a key concern for any single-center trial. Our trial enrolled only patients with heart failure with reduced ejection fraction at a single academic center. We agree that the original wording extended the findings too broadly. We have revised the Discussion to limit our conclusions to patients with heart failure with reduced ejection fraction treated at academic centers (page 14, lines 301–306) and added single-center design as a limitation (page 15, lines 322–325).
