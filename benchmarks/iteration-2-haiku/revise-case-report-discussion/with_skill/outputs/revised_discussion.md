## Discussion

Euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis (euDKA) is an uncommon but potentially life-threatening complication of sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitor therapy [CITATION NEEDED: incidence of euDKA with SGLT2 inhibitors]. SGLT2 inhibitors, now used for type 2 diabetes, heart failure, and chronic kidney disease, increase glycosuria, reduce insulin requirements, and enhance ketogenesis. Surgical stress, prolonged fasting, and reduced carbohydrate intake are precipitating factors in the perioperative period.

The reported increase in DKA risk with SGLT2 inhibitors is up to three-fold [CITATION NEEDED: source and comparator group]. The absence of marked hyperglycemia can delay the diagnosis. In our patient, nausea, vomiting, and abdominal discomfort on POD 1 were attributed to postoperative ileus and opioid analgesia, and capillary glucose was 182 mg/dL. The first abnormal venous blood gas was recorded that evening, and the diagnosis was made the next morning, about 14 hours later. Ketones should be measured in any patient taking these agents who presents with nausea, vomiting, or malaise.

She fasted for 14 hours before surgery. Her last empagliflozin dose was taken the morning before surgery. The FDA recommends that empagliflozin be discontinued at least 3 days before scheduled surgery [5]. The interval between her last dose and surgery was therefore shorter than recommended; this likely contributed to euDKA. Because surgical stress and fasting were also present, a single case cannot separate their contributions. The anion gap closed after 38 hours of insulin and dextrose therapy, and empagliflozin was discontinued permanently. Whether earlier recognition would have shortened this course cannot be determined from one patient.

## Conclusion

Ketones should be measured in patients taking an SGLT2 inhibitor who present with nausea, vomiting, or malaise after surgery, even without marked hyperglycemia. In this patient, a capillary glucose of 182 mg/dL did not exclude DKA.
