# Draft sections: PPI use and CDI in older adults admitted with CAP

## Results: primary and secondary outcomes

Of the 6,214 adults aged 65 years or older admitted with community-acquired pneumonia (CAP), 2,377 were taking a proton pump inhibitor (PPI) at admission and 3,837 were not. [DATA NEEDED: confirm whether the 312 patients (5.0%) excluded for missing medication reconciliation are additional to the 6,214 analyzed. 312 of 6,214 is 5.0%; 312 of 6,526 is 4.8%.] Within 90 days of discharge, Clostridioides difficile infection (CDI) occurred in 4.1% of PPI users and 2.6% of non-users (adjusted HR, 1.48; 95% CI, 1.12-1.96) [DATA NEEDED: number of CDI events in each group]. PPI use was not associated with 90-day all-cause mortality (11.8% vs 11.2%; adjusted HR, 1.03; 95% CI, 0.88-1.21) or 30-day readmission (17.9% vs 16.4%; adjusted HR, 1.06; 95% CI, 0.94-1.20). In the prespecified sex subgroup analysis, the adjusted HR for CDI was 1.81 (95% CI, 1.24-2.64) in men and 1.21 (95% CI, 0.81-1.80) in women. The test for interaction by sex was not significant (P = .14).

## Discussion (paragraphs 1 and 2)

Our finding that home PPI use at admission was associated with a higher risk of CDI within 90 days of discharge agrees in direction with the pooled estimate in Kwok et al, a meta-analysis of observational studies. That analysis reported an odds ratio of 1.74 (95% CI, 1.47-2.85) for PPI use and incident CDI (DOI: 10.1038/ajg.2012.108). Our adjusted HR of 1.48 is smaller, but the two measures come from different designs and populations, and the pooled estimate was marked by heterogeneity (I² = 85%). The same review pooled three studies of recurrent CDI and reported an odds ratio of 2.51 (95% CI, 1.16-5.44). Evidence on recurrence is mixed. In a retrospective inpatient cohort of 894 adults with incident CDI, PPI receipt during CDI treatment was not associated with recurrence (HR, 0.82; 95% CI, 0.58-1.16) [ADDED: verified, DOI 10.1038/ajg.2013.333]. A 2014 review by the same group summarized the PPI association as present for incident infection and absent for recurrent infection [ADDED: verified, DOI 10.1016/j.cll.2014.08.008]. Our outcome did not distinguish first from recurrent episodes [CONFIRM], so these data cannot resolve the question. Acid suppression has been proposed to increase risk by letting spores survive gastric passage and by altering colonic microbiota. Our data do not address either pathway.

The association was larger in men (adjusted HR, 1.81) than in women (adjusted HR, 1.21), but the interaction test was not significant (P = .14), the estimate for women includes the null, and the sex-specific estimates are imprecise. These data do not establish a sex difference, and the cohort cannot explain one. The subgroup result is hypothesis-generating until replicated. PPI use was not associated with 90-day mortality or 30-day readmission. The CDI association was therefore not accompanied by a detectable difference in either outcome. This analysis was not designed to test whether CDI contributed to mortality or readmission, and the upper confidence limits (1.21 for mortality and 1.20 for readmission) leave room for meaningful increases.

## Limitations

This retrospective observational study cannot exclude residual confounding. The indication for PPI therapy (reflux, dyspepsia, or gastrointestinal bleeding) was not adjusted for, though it may itself relate to CDI risk. Pneumonia severity at presentation was also not adjusted for. Antibiotic exposure was adjusted only by class during the index stay; duration and other antibiotic use were not included. PPI exposure reflects one medication reconciliation at admission, so duration, dose, adherence, and post-discharge changes are unknown. This misclassification would probably bias the estimate toward the null, although its direction is not certain here. CDI was identified only from laboratory tests within this health system, so infections diagnosed elsewhere were missed, and testing intensity may differ by exposure group. Death within 90 days competes with CDI detection (mortality 11.2% to 11.8%), and the primary Cox model appears to have censored deaths [CONFIRM]; a competing-risks analysis is needed. The 312 excluded patients (5.0%) may differ from those included in a direction we cannot determine. The sex-subgroup estimates are imprecise and the interaction test was not significant, so they are hypothesis-generating only. The cohort comes from a single health system.
