## Discussion

In this retrospective cohort of 1,146 patients with dementia who underwent hip fracture surgery, mobilization within 24 hours of surgery was associated with lower 30-day mortality (6.2% vs 9.8%; adjusted odds ratio, 0.61; 95% CI, 0.39–0.95) and a shorter length of stay (median 8 vs 11 days). Because the timing of mobilization was not assigned, these associations may partly reflect which patients were mobilized early. Patients who are medically stable and were walking before the fracture are more likely both to get out of bed on the first postoperative day and to survive to 30 days. The similar delirium incidence does not suggest that early mobilization precipitated delirium in this population. The study was not powered to detect small differences in delirium, however, and delirium during admission is a narrow measure of cognitive outcome; we did not assess cognition or function after discharge. We also cannot say from these data whether early mobilization is feasible or beneficial for patients with severe dementia. In patients with dementia, mobilization within 24 hours of hip fracture surgery was associated with lower 30-day mortality and a shorter hospital stay, with no increase in delirium. These findings do not support delaying mobilization on the basis of dementia alone. A prospective study with complete baseline functional data, stratified by dementia severity, is needed to determine whether the association reflects a causal effect.
