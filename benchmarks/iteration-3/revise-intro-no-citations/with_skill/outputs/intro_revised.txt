Introduction

Atrial fibrillation (AF) is the most common sustained cardiac arrhythmia, and older adults bear the greatest burden of the disease [CITATION NEEDED: age-specific AF prevalence]. Smartwatches equipped with photoplethysmography (PPG) and single-lead electrocardiography (ECG) can screen for AF. In validation studies, smartwatch algorithms detected AF with high sensitivity ([DATA NEEDED: reported range]) and specificity ([DATA NEEDED: reported range]) [CITATION NEEDED: validation studies, with design, population, and participant age]. Earlier detection of AF may reduce stroke risk [CITATION NEEDED: outcome evidence for screen-detected AF].

Whether these accuracy estimates hold in older adults is uncertain [DATA NEEDED: number or proportion of participants aged 75 years or older in prior validation studies]. Tremor, peripheral vascular disease, and frequent ectopy are common in older adults and may affect signal quality [CITATION NEEDED: effect of these conditions on PPG and ECG recordings], so the devices need to be validated in this population.

We conducted a prospective study of the diagnostic accuracy of a commercially available smartwatch for detecting AF in adults aged 75 years and older, with 14-day continuous patch monitoring as the reference standard.
