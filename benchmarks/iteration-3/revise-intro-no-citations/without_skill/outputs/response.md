**What changed**

- **Cut promotional and filler phrasing.** I removed "transform the landscape," "critical gap," "comprehensive," and "not only fills an important gap... but also provides valuable insights that will... pave the way." Reviewers flag this kind of language, and the closing sentence claimed impact before you had shown any results. The intro now ends on the study objective, which is the usual endpoint for a diagnostic accuracy paper (STARD item 4).
- **Made the age argument earlier and more specific.** The draft didn't say why 75+ matters until paragraph 2. Paragraph 1 now sets it up: prevalence rises with age, this group carries a disproportionate share of AF-related stroke, and many cases are only found after a complication.
- **Toned down the outcomes claim.** "Early detection may lead to improved outcomes, including reduced stroke risk" is more than the screening trials support. Most found more diagnoses and more anticoagulation, but stroke reductions were small or not significant. Overstating that will draw reviewer criticism. The revision states the uncertainty, then argues that any screening strategy still needs known accuracy in the population being screened. That keeps your rationale intact without overclaiming.
- **Spelled out the mechanism for the comorbidities.** Each one is now tied to how it would affect the device: tremor degrades the PPG signal, PVD and poor perfusion weaken the waveform, and ectopy produces irregular intervals that can be read as AF. I also named the likely consequences (lower sensitivity, more false positives, more unclassifiable readings) and added the spectrum-bias point. Spectrum bias is the methodological reason you can't borrow estimates from younger cohorts.
- **Replaced "poorly understood" with a specific, citable claim:** older adults have been underrepresented in validation cohorts.
- **Changed "single-lead ECG" to "user-initiated single-lead ECG"** so it's clear the ECG is a different mode from passive PPG.

**Placeholders to fill in**

- `[REF]` marks every spot that needs a citation. One of them says `[REF; add reported ranges]`. Giving actual sensitivity and specificity ranges from earlier studies works better than "high sensitivity and specificity."
- `[device name and algorithm/software version]`: STARD expects the index test to be identified exactly, including the software version.

**Assumptions and things to check**

- I assumed the index test is the watch's automated AF detection. If you're evaluating PPG notifications and the ECG app separately, or one of them alone, rewrite the objective sentence to say so.
- I kept the objective to sensitivity and specificity. If you also report PPV/NPV, the unclassifiable rate, or subgroups (for example, people with tremor or frequent ectopy), add them to that last sentence. Paragraph 3 already sets up those analyses.
- The underrepresentation claim needs a citation showing it, such as the age breakdown of the large smartwatch validation cohorts. If you can't find good support, change it to "few studies have specifically evaluated..."
- The intro is in past tense ("we conducted"), which fits a results manuscript. If this is going into a protocol paper, switch it to future tense.
- I used "ECG patch monitoring" for the reference standard. If the patch has a specific name or adjudication process (for example, review by blinded cardiologists), put that in Methods rather than here.
