## Discussion

Euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis (euDKA) is a rare but potentially fatal adverse effect of sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitors. These drugs lower plasma glucose by causing glycosuria, which reduces insulin requirements and promotes ketogenesis. Ketoacidosis can therefore develop while glucose is only modestly elevated. The risk of DKA has been reported to be up to three times higher in patients taking SGLT2 inhibitors [CITATION NEEDED]. Surgical stress, prolonged fasting, and reduced carbohydrate intake are recognized precipitants. Our patient had all three: she fasted for 14 hours before surgery and then developed nausea and vomiting on POD 1.

The near-normal glucose delayed the diagnosis. When her symptoms began on POD 1, her capillary glucose was 182 mg/dL, and the nausea, vomiting, and abdominal discomfort were attributed to postoperative ileus and opioid analgesia. That evening she became tachypneic, and a venous blood gas showed a high anion gap metabolic acidosis (pH 7.12, bicarbonate 9 mmol/L, anion gap 24 mmol/L) with a normal lactate. The diagnosis of euDKA was not made until the morning of POD 2, approximately 14 hours after this first abnormal gas, when a serum beta-hydroxybutyrate of 6.8 mmol/L confirmed ketoacidosis. Nausea, vomiting, and abdominal discomfort are expected after abdominal surgery, so they are easy to misattribute in a patient whose glucose is not markedly raised. In patients taking an SGLT2 inhibitor, these symptoms should prompt measurement of serum ketones regardless of the glucose level.

The US Food and Drug Administration recommends stopping empagliflozin at least 3 days before scheduled surgery [5]. Our patient was instructed to hold metformin on the morning of surgery, but empagliflozin was continued, and her last dose was taken the morning before the operation. Continued empagliflozin, combined with the prolonged preoperative fast and the stress of surgery, likely contributed to her ketoacidosis. She was treated with an intravenous insulin infusion and concurrent 10% dextrose, fluid resuscitation, and potassium replacement, and the anion gap closed after 38 hours. Empagliflozin was discontinued permanently, and she was discharged on POD 6 on metformin and basal insulin.

## Conclusion

Euglycemic DKA should be considered in any postoperative patient taking an SGLT2 inhibitor who develops nausea, vomiting, abdominal discomfort, or tachypnea. A modestly elevated glucose, such as this patient's 182 mg/dL, does not exclude the diagnosis, and serum ketones should be measured. Preoperative medication instructions for these patients should address the SGLT2 inhibitor specifically, in line with the FDA recommendation to stop empagliflozin at least 3 days before elective surgery.
