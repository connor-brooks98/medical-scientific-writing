The two Critical problems:

- **P1 s3, "Studies have shown that early norepinephrine improves survival."** This is a causal claim with no citation and no named study, and the trial evidence doesn't appear to support it. According to PubMed, the CENSER randomized trial (Permpikul et al., 2019, [doi:10.1164/rccm.201806-1034OC](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.201806-1034OC)) found better shock control at 6 hours but no difference in 28-day mortality (15.5% vs 21.9%; P = 0.15). It also works against your own rationale: if a survival benefit were established, there would be no gap to fill.
- **P2 s2, "Early norepinephrine therefore prevents death."** A retrospective association can't support "prevents" (confounding by indication, and bias from how the time window was defined). Your previous sentence already says "was associated with", which is correct.

Most of the other findings come from the same few habits. The opener is generic ("Sepsis remains a leading cause of death worldwide", "growing interest"). The evidence is unnamed and hedged four times over in s4. "This critical gap" refers to a gap the text never names. The last sentence promises results ("not only fill... but also pave the way") where the aim, design, and primary outcome should be.

The most useful change: make the gap between your retrospective association and the null randomized mortality result your rationale.
