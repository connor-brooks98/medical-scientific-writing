There are three problems to fix before you work on the wording:

1. **S6 makes a causal claim your data can't support.** A retrospective adjusted OR of 0.78 is an association. "Early norepinephrine therefore prevents death" is a causal and absolute claim, and reviewers will flag it first. It also contradicts the correct "was associated with" in the sentence before it.
2. **The argument undercuts your own study.** S3 says early norepinephrine "improves survival," and S6 says it "prevents death." If either were true, there would be no reason to run a prospective trial. Then S7 refers to "this critical gap," but no gap is ever stated. The real gap is the weakness of retrospective timing data (confounding by indication, time-zero definition, immortal-time bias). It belongs right after your cohort result.
3. **No citations and no aim.** Every claim from the literature in paragraph 1 has no source. S7 never says what the study is: no design, population, comparator, primary outcome, or hypothesis.

The rest is prose quality:

- **Stacked hedges in S4.** "suggests ... may potentially be associated ... in some patients" has five qualifiers on one claim.
- **S3 and S4 say the same thing.** S4 restates S3 from the opposite direction.
- **Boilerplate and repetition.** Phrases like "pivotal challenge in the evolving landscape," "growing interest," "comprehensive," and "pave the way for improved outcomes" add nothing. "Growing" and "gap" each appear twice.
- **Missing details in S5.** It needs its comparator and a definition of shock onset. Also be careful presenting an OR when the outcome (28-day mortality) is common.

S5 is the strongest sentence. It's specific, has a CI, and is correctly worded as an association, so keep its substance.
