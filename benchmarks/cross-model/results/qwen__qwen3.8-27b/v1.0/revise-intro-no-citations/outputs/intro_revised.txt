Introduction

Atrial fibrillation (AF) is the most common sustained cardiac arrhythmia, and its prevalence rises with age [CITATION NEEDED: age-specific AF prevalence in adults aged 75 years or older]. Detecting AF early may reduce stroke risk [CITATION NEEDED: evidence linking early AF detection to reduced stroke risk]. Photoplethysmography (PPG) and single-lead electrocardiography (ECG) are now embedded in commercially available smartwatches, and smartwatch-based algorithms have detected AF with high sensitivity and specificity in prior accuracy studies [CITATION NEEDED: named smartwatch AF detection studies, with sensitivity and specificity and populations].

However, the accuracy of these devices in adults aged 75 years or older remains poorly understood. In this population, comorbidities that may affect signal quality are common: tremor, peripheral vascular disease, and frequent ectopy.
