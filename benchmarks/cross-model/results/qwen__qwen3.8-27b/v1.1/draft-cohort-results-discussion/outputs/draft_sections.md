## Results

Among 6,214 adults aged 65 years or older admitted with community-acquired pneumonia, 2,377 used a proton pump inhibitor (PPI) at home at admission and 3,837 did not; 312 patients (5.0%) were excluded because of missing medication reconciliation data. Clostridioides difficile infection (CDI) within 90 days of discharge developed in 4.1% of PPI users and 2.6% of non-users; the adjusted hazard ratio (HR) was 1.48 (95% CI, 1.12–1.96). All-cause mortality within 90 days of discharge was 11.8% in PPI users and 11.2% in non-users; the adjusted HR was 1.03 (95% CI, 0.88–1.21). Readmission within 30 days of discharge was 17.9% in PPI users and 16.4% in non-users; the adjusted HR was 1.06 (95% CI, 0.94–1.20). In the prespecified sex subgroups, the adjusted HRs for 90-day CDI were 1.81 (95% CI, 1.24–2.64) in men and 1.21 (95% CI, 0.81–1.80) in women; the interaction P value was 0.14.

## Discussion

In this cohort of adults 65 years or older hospitalized with community-acquired pneumonia, home PPI use at admission was associated with a higher hazard of CDI within 90 days of discharge (adjusted HR, 1.48; 95% CI, 1.12–1.96). The estimate is similar in magnitude to the association between PPI use and CDI in a 2012 meta-analysis by Kwok et al (odds ratio, approximately 1.74) [REF: Kwok 2012]. It is also directionally consistent with the association between PPI use and recurrent CDI reported by Freedberg et al [REF: Freedberg 2015]. The setting shapes the interpretation: all participants were hospitalized for pneumonia, an infection that typically requires systemic antibiotics, and systemic antibiotics are a well-established driver of CDI. The PPI association persisted after adjustment for the antibiotic class given during the index admission, so the estimate is not explained by that measured exposure. Dose and duration of antibiotics were not captured.

The association with CDI was not accompanied by differences in the secondary outcomes. Ninety-day all-cause mortality (adjusted HR, 1.03; 95% CI, 0.88–1.21) and 30-day readmission (adjusted HR, 1.06; 95% CI, 0.94–1.20) were similar in the two groups. The association was stronger in men (adjusted HR, 1.81; 95% CI, 1.24–2.64) than in women (adjusted HR, 1.21; 95% CI, 0.81–1.80), but the prespecified interaction P value of 0.14 does not establish a difference between sexes. One plausible mechanism is that acid suppression promotes colonization of the gastrointestinal tract with C. difficile; this study did not measure colonization, so the mechanism remains untested. The implication for practice is uncertain. An observational association cannot show that withholding PPIs prevents CDI; a pragmatic trial randomizing hospitalized older adults at elevated CDI risk to PPI continuation versus deprescribing, with CDI as an endpoint, would test whether the association is causal.

## Limitations

Several limitations apply. PPI exposure was ascertained from the medication reconciliation at admission only; duration, dose, and adherence of prior use were not measured, and use after admission was not captured, so exposure misclassification is possible, most likely attenuating the estimate. CDI was identified through inpatient and outpatient laboratories within one health system; infections identified outside the system were missed, which would tend to dilute the association. Residual confounding, including confounding by indication, cannot be excluded, because the conditions for which PPIs were prescribed were not adjusted for. Antibiotic exposure was captured as the class received during the index admission only. Finally, the study was conducted in one academic health system among adults 65 years or older hospitalized with community-acquired pneumonia, which limits generalizability to younger patients, to community-dwelling adults, and to other acute hospitalizations.
