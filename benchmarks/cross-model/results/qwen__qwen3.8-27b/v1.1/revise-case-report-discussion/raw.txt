## Discussion

This patient developed euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis (euDKA) after an uncomplicated laparoscopic cholecystectomy while continuing empagliflozin. On the evening of postoperative day 1, after she became tachypneic, a venous blood gas showed pH 7.12, bicarbonate 9 mmol/L, and an anion gap of 24 mmol/L. The diagnosis of euDKA was made on the morning of postoperative day 2, about 14 hours after the first abnormal gas, and the serum beta-hydroxybutyrate was 6.8 mmol/L.

The delay reflected two features of the presentation. Her nausea, vomiting, and abdominal discomfort on postoperative day 1 were attributed to postoperative ileus and opioid analgesia, and her capillary glucose of 182 mg/dL was not high enough to raise suspicion of DKA. Empagliflozin, like other SGLT2 inhibitors, promotes glycosuria, reduces insulin requirements, and enhances ketogenesis, so ketoacidosis can develop without marked hyperglycemia. In this patient, the drug effect coincided with the recognized precipitants of surgical stress, the 14-hour preoperative fast, and reduced carbohydrate intake.

The risk of DKA has been reported to be up to three-fold higher in patients receiving SGLT2 inhibitors [CITATION NEEDED: source for the up to three-fold risk estimate]. The FDA recommends discontinuing empagliflozin at least 3 days before scheduled surgery [5]. In this patient the drug was continued until the day before surgery, and that likely contributed to the development of euDKA. The acidosis resolved with intravenous insulin and 10% dextrose, and the anion gap closed after 38 hours. A single case cannot separate the contribution of the SGLT2 inhibitor from that of the fast and the operation.

## Conclusion

In a patient taking an SGLT2 inhibitor, a capillary glucose of 182 mg/dL did not exclude diabetic ketoacidosis, and the diagnosis was delayed about 14 hours. This case supports two practices: discontinuing empagliflozin at least 3 days before scheduled surgery, as the FDA recommends [5], and measuring ketones in postoperative patients on SGLT2 inhibitors who have persistent nausea, vomiting, or malaise, regardless of the glucose level.
