Importance: Short sleep has been associated with cardiovascular disease, but its association with incident atrial fibrillation (AF) in older adults is uncertain.

Objective: To examine whether self-reported sleep duration is associated with incident AF in adults aged 65 years or older.

Design, Setting, and Participants: This prospective cohort study enrolled 4,912 community-dwelling adults without AF at 4 US sites between 2009 and 2012. Follow-up continued through 2022.

Exposure: Self-reported habitual sleep duration, categorized as 6 hours or less, 7 to 8 hours (reference), or 9 hours or more.

Main Outcomes and Measures: Incident AF, ascertained from study electrocardiograms, hospital discharge codes, and Medicare claims.

Results: During a median follow-up of 9.8 years, 781 participants developed AF. Compared with participants who reported 7 to 8 hours of sleep, those who reported 6 hours or less had a higher hazard of AF (adjusted hazard ratio, 1.29; 95% CI, 1.08–1.54). For participants who reported 9 hours or more, the adjusted hazard ratio was 1.07 (95% CI, 0.86–1.33) relative to the same reference group. After additional adjustment for obstructive sleep apnea symptoms, the hazard ratio for 6 hours or less was 1.18 (95% CI, 0.98–1.42).

Conclusions and Relevance: In this cohort of older adults, participants who reported 6 hours or less of sleep had a higher hazard of incident AF than those who reported 7 to 8 hours. After adjustment for obstructive sleep apnea symptoms, the association was weaker and the 95% CI included 1.
