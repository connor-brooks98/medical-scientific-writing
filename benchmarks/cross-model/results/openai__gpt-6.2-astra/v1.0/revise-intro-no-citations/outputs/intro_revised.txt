Introduction

Smartwatches can use photoplethysmography (PPG) to identify an irregular pulse and single-lead electrocardiography (ECG) to record the rhythm. Although validation studies have reported high sensitivity and specificity for atrial fibrillation (AF) [CITATION NEEDED: validation studies, populations, and accuracy estimates], it is unclear whether those estimates apply to adults aged 75 years and older [CITATION NEEDED: age-specific accuracy data or a review documenting their limits].

Tremor and peripheral vascular disease may impair PPG signal quality, whereas frequent ectopy can produce an irregular pulse even when AF is absent [CITATION NEEDED: effects of these conditions on PPG recordings and AF classification]. Both signal quality and the ability to distinguish AF from other irregular rhythms matter when assessing smartwatch accuracy in this age group.

In this prospective diagnostic accuracy study, we evaluated a commercially available smartwatch for detecting AF in adults aged 75 years and older, using 14-day continuous patch monitoring as the reference standard.
