## Discussion

In this retrospective cohort of 1,146 patients with dementia undergoing hip fracture surgery, 30-day mortality was lower among patients mobilized within 24 hours of surgery than among those mobilized later (6.2% vs 9.8%; adjusted odds ratio, 0.61; 95% CI, 0.39–0.95). Median length of stay was also shorter in the early mobilization group (8 vs 11 days). Patients who were clinically stable soon after surgery may have been more likely to mobilize early and to have better outcomes. The associations therefore cannot be attributed to mobilization timing alone.

Delirium incidence was similar in the early and later mobilization groups (31% vs 29%). This finding does not establish that delirium risk was equivalent between groups or address cognitive outcomes beyond delirium.

Preoperative ambulatory status was not recorded for 18% of patients. These patients cannot be assumed to have been nonambulatory. The missing data limit assessment of whether baseline mobility contributed to differences in mobilization timing and outcomes.

The mortality, length-of-stay, and delirium findings support further evaluation of early mobilization after hip fracture surgery in patients with dementia, but they do not establish its safety or benefit. Prospective studies that document baseline mobility, dementia severity, and reasons for delayed mobilization could help distinguish the effects of mobilization timing from differences in patients’ readiness to participate.
