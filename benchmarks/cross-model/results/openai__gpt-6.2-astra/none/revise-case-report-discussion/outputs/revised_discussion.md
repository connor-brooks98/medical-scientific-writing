## Discussion

On postoperative day 1, the patient’s nausea, vomiting, and abdominal discomfort were initially attributed to ileus and opioid analgesia. Her capillary glucose was only 182 mg/dL. When she developed tachypnea that evening, venous blood gas testing showed a pH of 7.12 and a bicarbonate concentration of 9 mmol/L. An anion gap of 24 mmol/L and a serum beta-hydroxybutyrate concentration of 6.8 mmol/L supported the diagnosis of euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis (euDKA), while lactate was normal at 1.4 mmol/L. The diagnosis was made approximately 14 hours after the first abnormal blood gas.

Sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitors promote urinary glucose loss, lowering blood glucose and reducing insulin requirements even as ketone production increases. Although DKA is rare, studies have reported up to a threefold increase in risk among patients taking these agents. Surgical stress, fasting, and reduced carbohydrate intake can further favor ketogenesis. In this patient, perioperative empagliflozin exposure and a 14-hour preoperative fast likely contributed to ketoacidosis despite the absence of marked hyperglycemia.

The FDA recommends withholding empagliflozin for at least 3 days before scheduled surgery [5]. This patient took her last dose the morning before surgery. Once euDKA was recognized, she received intravenous insulin with 10% dextrose, fluid resuscitation, and potassium replacement. The anion gap closed after 38 hours.

## Conclusion

Preoperative medication instructions should specify when to stop empagliflozin. In a patient with recent SGLT2 inhibitor exposure, postoperative nausea or vomiting warrants ketone testing even when blood glucose is only modestly elevated. Unexplained tachypnea or a high anion gap should prompt evaluation for euDKA without waiting for the glucose concentration to rise.
