# Audit of introduction.txt

| Location | Quoted text | Pattern ID | Severity | Suggested fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ¶1, sentences 1–2 | “Sepsis remains a leading cause of death worldwide”; “there has been growing interest” | D6 (generic preamble), W1 (inflated wording) | Major | Start closer to the timing decision in septic shock. Neither the global burden of sepsis nor interest in vasopressors explains why the timing of norepinephrine is uncertain. Keep the mortality-burden claim only if it does that work, and cite it if retained. |
| ¶1, sentence 3 | “Studies have shown that early norepinephrine improves survival” | D2 (unnamed evidence), S6 (causal wording) | Critical | Identify and cite the studies. Check how each defined “early,” what it used as the comparator, and when it measured survival. Use a causal verb only if the design and endpoint support it. As written, the sentence makes the survival question sound settled. |
| ¶1, sentence 4 | “delayed vasopressor therapy may potentially be associated with worse outcomes in some patients” | D2 (unnamed evidence), D9 (possible restatement), S6 (stacked hedges), S13 (missing specifics) | Major | Decide whether this is distinct evidence or the preceding claim viewed from the opposite direction. If distinct, cite it and identify the vasopressor, definition of delay, population, and outcome. “Vasopressor therapy” is broader than norepinephrine; “worse outcomes” need not mean mortality. “May potentially” resolves neither ambiguity. |
| ¶2, sentence 1 | “norepinephrine started within 1 hour of shock onset was associated with lower 28-day mortality” | S13 (missing comparator) | Major | Name the reference group for the adjusted odds ratio; do not assume it comprised everyone treated after 1 hour. Keep the cohort size, timing threshold, endpoint, estimate, and CI. Identify the source of the institutional analysis, citing it if published. |
| ¶2, sentence 2 | “Early norepinephrine therefore prevents death in septic shock.” | S6 (causal overclaim), D9 (restatement) | Critical | Remove the causal conclusion. The preceding sentence already states what the retrospective analysis found. Differences between patients treated earlier and those in the reference group could account for some of the association despite adjustment. |
| ¶2, sentence 3, rationale | “To address this critical gap”; “fill an important gap in the literature” | D4 (unsupported gap claim), D9 (repetition) | Major | Identify the specific uncertainty left by the prior evidence and the institutional cohort. If the uncertainty is causal, explain which feature of the new design addresses it. Prospective observation alone does not remove confounding. |
| ¶2, sentence 3, study aim | “a comprehensive prospective study that will not only fill an important gap in the literature but also pave the way for improved outcomes” | S13 (unspecified comparison and endpoint), D5 (promotional ending) | Major | End with the study question. Specify the actual design, population, timing comparison, and primary endpoint. Do not assume the prospective study uses the retrospective study’s cutoff or endpoint, or promise that conducting it will improve outcomes. |

## Five revisions to make first

1. Remove the claim that the retrospective association establishes prevention of death.
2. Check the survival claim against specific studies and calibrate its wording to their designs.
3. Name the reference group for the institutional cohort’s adjusted odds ratio.
4. State what remains unknown, then give the prospective study’s question and design.
5. Cut the generic opening and retain the delayed-treatment sentence only if it adds distinct, supported evidence.
