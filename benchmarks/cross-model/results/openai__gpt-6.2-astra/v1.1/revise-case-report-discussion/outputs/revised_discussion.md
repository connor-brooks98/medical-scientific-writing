## Discussion

On postoperative day (POD) 1, the surgical team initially attributed the patient's nausea, vomiting, and abdominal discomfort to postoperative ileus and opioid analgesia. Her capillary glucose was 182 mg/dL. By that evening, she was tachypneic and had a high anion gap metabolic acidosis (venous pH 7.12, bicarbonate 9 mmol/L, anion gap 24 mmol/L). Lactate was 1.4 mmol/L. The serum beta-hydroxybutyrate concentration of 6.8 mmol/L supported the diagnosis of euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis (euDKA), which was made on the morning of POD 2, approximately 14 hours after the first abnormal blood gas.

Sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitors promote urinary glucose loss, which can limit the rise in blood glucose during ketoacidosis. Although euDKA is uncommon, SGLT2 inhibitor use has been associated with up to a three-fold increase in the risk of DKA [CITATION NEEDED: incidence of euDKA and source, population, and comparator for the DKA risk estimate]. Lower insulin requirements during SGLT2 inhibitor therapy, reduced carbohydrate intake during fasting, and surgical stress can favor ketogenesis. This patient fasted for 14 hours before surgery and took her last dose of empagliflozin the morning before the operation. The FDA recommends discontinuing empagliflozin at least 3 days before scheduled surgery [5]. Recent empagliflozin exposure may have contributed to euDKA, although its contribution cannot be separated from that of fasting and surgical stress in this case.

The patient received intravenous insulin with concurrent 10% dextrose, fluids, and potassium replacement. The anion gap closed after 38 hours. Empagliflozin was permanently discontinued, and she was discharged on POD 6 on metformin and basal insulin.

## Conclusion

For patients taking empagliflozin who are scheduled for elective surgery, preoperative medication review should establish when the last dose was taken. After surgery, nausea or vomiting accompanied by unexplained tachypnea or high anion gap metabolic acidosis warrants measurement of serum beta-hydroxybutyrate in patients with recent SGLT2 inhibitor exposure, even when blood glucose is only modestly elevated.
