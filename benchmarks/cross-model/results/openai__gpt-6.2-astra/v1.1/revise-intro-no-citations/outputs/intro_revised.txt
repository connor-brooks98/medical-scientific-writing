Introduction

Smartwatches can use photoplethysmography (PPG) to identify an irregular pulse or single-lead electrocardiography (ECG) to record the cardiac rhythm. The accuracy of smartwatch algorithms for detecting atrial fibrillation (AF) is less well characterized in adults aged 75 years and older [CITATION NEEDED: validation studies reporting sensitivity, specificity, and the ages of participants].

AF is common in this age group [CITATION NEEDED: AF prevalence in adults aged 75 years and older]. Tremor may introduce motion artifact, and peripheral vascular disease may affect the quality of the PPG signal [CITATION NEEDED: effects of tremor and peripheral vascular disease on wrist recordings]. Frequent ectopy poses a different problem: even when the signal is clear, an irregular rhythm may be misclassified as AF [CITATION NEEDED: ectopy and false-positive smartwatch AF classifications].

In this prospective diagnostic accuracy study, we evaluated a commercially available smartwatch for detecting AF in adults aged 75 years and older, using 14-day continuous patch monitoring as the reference standard.
