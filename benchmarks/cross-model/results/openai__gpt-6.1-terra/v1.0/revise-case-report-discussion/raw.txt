## Discussion

Sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitors are used to treat type 2 diabetes, heart failure, and chronic kidney disease. They promote glycosuria, reduce insulin requirements, and enhance ketogenesis. Studies have reported an increase in diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) risk of up to threefold in patients receiving SGLT2 inhibitors [CITATION NEEDED: source, comparator, population, and risk estimate].

In this patient, empagliflozin was continued until the morning before surgery. Preoperative fasting and surgical stress may have contributed to the development of euglycemic DKA (euDKA). Her initial nausea, vomiting, and abdominal discomfort were attributed to postoperative ileus and opioid analgesia. Glucose was 182 mg/dL, and the diagnosis was made approximately 14 hours after the first abnormal blood gas. The absence of marked hyperglycemia can make DKA less apparent; ketone and acid-base testing may help identify it in postoperative patients taking SGLT2 inhibitors who develop compatible symptoms.

The FDA recommends discontinuing empagliflozin at least 3 days before scheduled surgery [5]. After diagnosis, the patient received intravenous insulin with dextrose, fluids, and potassium. The anion gap closed after 38 hours, and empagliflozin was permanently discontinued.

## Conclusion

In postoperative patients taking SGLT2 inhibitors, nausea, vomiting, or tachypnea may warrant assessment for ketoacidosis even without marked hyperglycemia. Empagliflozin should be stopped before scheduled surgery in accordance with FDA recommendations [5].
