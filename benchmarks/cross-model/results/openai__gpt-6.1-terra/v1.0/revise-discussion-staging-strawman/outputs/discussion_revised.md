## Discussion

In this retrospective cohort of 1,146 patients with dementia, mobilization within 24 hours of hip fracture surgery was associated with lower 30-day mortality (6.2% vs 9.8%; adjusted odds ratio, 0.61; 95% CI, 0.39–0.95) and shorter length of stay (median 8 vs 11 days) compared with later mobilization. Delirium incidence did not differ between groups (31% vs 29%). The delirium result does not rule out a difference in cognitive outcomes.

These associations do not establish that early mobilization caused the differences in mortality or length of stay. Patients who were well enough to mobilize early may have differed from those mobilized later in ways not captured by the analysis. Information on preoperative ambulatory status was not recorded for 18% of patients; their missing status cannot be assumed to indicate that they were nonambulatory. Depending on how the missing data were handled, differences in preoperative mobility could contribute to residual confounding.
