Introduction

Atrial fibrillation (AF) is the most common sustained cardiac arrhythmia and affects millions of people worldwide [CITATION NEEDED: AF prevalence]. Smartwatches can use photoplethysmography (PPG) and single-lead electrocardiography (ECG) to detect irregular rhythms. Studies have reported high sensitivity and specificity for smartwatch-based AF detection [CITATION NEEDED: diagnostic accuracy studies]. Earlier detection may allow earlier evaluation and stroke prevention, although whether smartwatch screening reduces stroke risk requires evaluation [CITATION NEEDED: evidence on screening and clinical outcomes].

The accuracy of smartwatch-based AF detection in adults aged 75 years and older is less well understood [CITATION NEEDED: evidence on validation in this age group]. Older adults bear a large burden of AF [CITATION NEEDED: age-specific AF burden] and may have tremor, peripheral vascular disease, or frequent ectopy that can affect signal quality or rhythm interpretation [CITATION NEEDED: evidence for these effects]. These factors may affect device performance in this population.

We conducted a prospective study to evaluate the diagnostic accuracy of a commercially available smartwatch for detecting AF in adults aged 75 years and older, using 14-day continuous patch monitoring as the reference standard.
