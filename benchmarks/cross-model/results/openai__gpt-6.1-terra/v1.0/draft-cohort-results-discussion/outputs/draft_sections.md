## Results

Among 6214 adults aged 65 years or older admitted with community-acquired pneumonia from 2016 to 2023, 2377 used a proton pump inhibitor (PPI) at admission and 3837 did not. Within 90 days of discharge, *Clostridioides difficile* infection occurred in 4.1% of PPI users and 2.6% of nonusers (adjusted hazard ratio [aHR], 1.48; 95% CI, 1.12-1.96). The model was adjusted for age, sex, Charlson comorbidity index, hospitalization in the preceding 12 months, and antibiotic class during the index stay. Ninety-day all-cause mortality was 11.8% among PPI users and 11.2% among nonusers (aHR, 1.03; 95% CI, 0.88-1.21). Thirty-day readmission was 17.9% among PPI users and 16.4% among nonusers (aHR, 1.06; 95% CI, 0.94-1.20). In prespecified subgroup analyses, the aHR for *C difficile* infection was 1.81 (95% CI, 1.24-2.64) among men and 1.21 (95% CI, 0.81-1.80) among women; the interaction was not statistically significant (P = .14).

## Discussion

In this retrospective cohort of older adults admitted with community-acquired pneumonia, home PPI use at admission was associated with a higher rate of *C difficile* infection within 90 days of discharge. The estimates for 90-day mortality and 30-day readmission were closer to the null, with confidence intervals that included both lower and higher rates among PPI users. The association with *C difficile* infection differed numerically by sex, but the interaction test did not provide evidence that the association differed between men and women.

The association with *C difficile* infection is consistent with the meta-analysis by Kwok et al., which reported an approximately 1.74-fold higher odds of infection among PPI users [CITATION NEEDED: verify reference and estimate]. Freedberg et al. also reported an association between PPI use and recurrent *C difficile* infection [CITATION NEEDED: verify reference and outcome]. Recurrent infection is a different outcome from the infection outcome examined here, and differences in populations and study designs limit direct comparison of the estimates.

## Limitations

This study has limitations. Its single-health-system setting may limit applicability to other populations. PPI exposure was measured from medication reconciliation at admission; duration of use and adherence were unknown. *C difficile* infection was ascertained through inpatient and outpatient laboratory testing within the health system, so infections identified elsewhere may have been missed. A total of 312 patients (5.0%) were excluded because medication reconciliation was missing; whether their exclusion introduced selection bias is unknown. Although the Cox model adjusted for measured characteristics, confounding by PPI indication or other unmeasured factors may remain. These limitations preclude a causal interpretation of the association.
