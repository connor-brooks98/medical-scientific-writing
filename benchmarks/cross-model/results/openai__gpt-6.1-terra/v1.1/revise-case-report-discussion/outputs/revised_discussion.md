## Discussion

This patient developed euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis (euDKA) after laparoscopic cholecystectomy while taking empagliflozin. SGLT2 inhibitors promote glycosuria, reduce insulin requirements, and increase ketogenesis. The risk of diabetic ketoacidosis has been reported to be up to three-fold higher in patients receiving these drugs [CITATION NEEDED: risk estimate and comparator]. Surgical stress, fasting, and reduced carbohydrate intake can also contribute to ketoacidosis.

The diagnosis was not apparent when nausea, vomiting, and abdominal discomfort developed on postoperative day (POD) 1; these symptoms were attributed to ileus and opioid analgesia. Her glucose was 182 mg/dL. Later that day, tachypnea prompted a venous blood gas, which showed a pH of 7.12, bicarbonate of 9 mmol/L, and an anion gap of 24 mmol/L. Lactate was 1.4 mmol/L. Serum beta-hydroxybutyrate was 6.8 mmol/L, and euDKA was diagnosed on the morning of POD 2, approximately 14 hours after the first abnormal blood gas. This course shows how the absence of marked hyperglycemia can complicate recognition of ketoacidosis.

The FDA recommends stopping empagliflozin at least 3 days before scheduled surgery [5]. In this case, the last dose was taken the morning before surgery, and the patient fasted for 14 hours preoperatively. Continued empagliflozin use, fasting, and surgical stress may have contributed to euDKA, although their individual contributions cannot be determined.

After diagnosis, she received intravenous insulin with concurrent 10% dextrose, fluids, and potassium. The anion gap closed after 38 hours. Empagliflozin was permanently discontinued, and she was discharged on POD 6 on metformin and basal insulin.

## Conclusion

In postoperative patients taking SGLT2 inhibitors, ketoacidosis can occur without marked hyperglycemia. Nausea, vomiting, or unexplained anion-gap acidosis in this setting should prompt consideration of ketone testing.
