## Results

Among 6214 adults aged 65 years or older admitted with community-acquired pneumonia from 2016 through 2023, 2377 used a proton pump inhibitor (PPI) at admission and 3837 did not. A total of 312 patients (5.0%) were excluded because medication reconciliation data were missing. Within 90 days of discharge, *Clostridioides difficile* infection (CDI) occurred in 4.1% of PPI users and 2.6% of nonusers. PPI use was associated with CDI after adjustment for age, sex, Charlson index, hospitalization in the prior 12 months, and antibiotic class during the index stay (adjusted hazard ratio [aHR], 1.48; 95% CI, 1.12-1.96). For 90-day all-cause mortality, the corresponding proportions were 11.8% and 11.2% (aHR, 1.03; 95% CI, 0.88-1.21); for 30-day readmission, they were 17.9% and 16.4% (aHR, 1.06; 95% CI, 0.94-1.20). In prespecified analyses, the aHR for CDI was 1.81 (95% CI, 1.24-2.64) among men and 1.21 (95% CI, 0.81-1.80) among women; the interaction test did not indicate a difference by sex (P = 0.14).

## Discussion

In this retrospective cohort of older adults admitted with community-acquired pneumonia, home PPI use at admission was associated with a higher hazard of CDI within 90 days of discharge. The confidence intervals for the associations with 90-day mortality and 30-day readmission included no difference. Although the CDI estimates differed between men and women, the interaction test did not support a conclusion that the association differed by sex.

Kwok et al. reported an association between PPI use and CDI in a meta-analysis (odds ratio, approximately 1.74) [CITATION NEEDED: verify Kwok et al., 2012, *American Journal of Gastroenterology*, and the estimate]. Freedberg et al. reported an association between PPI use and recurrent CDI [CITATION NEEDED: verify Freedberg et al., 2015, *Clinical Infectious Diseases*, and the finding]. The present analysis examined CDI after hospitalization for pneumonia; differences in populations, outcomes, and effect measures limit direct comparison with those reports.

## Limitations

This study has limitations. Its retrospective, single-health-system design limits generalizability and leaves the association susceptible to confounding, including confounding by the indication for PPI use. PPI exposure was measured at admission only; duration and adherence were unknown. CDI ascertainment relied on inpatient and outpatient laboratory records within the health system, so infections identified elsewhere could have been missed. Exclusion of patients with missing medication reconciliation data may have introduced selection bias if their exposure or outcomes differed from those of included patients. Death before CDI ascertainment is a competing event that may affect interpretation of the CDI association.
