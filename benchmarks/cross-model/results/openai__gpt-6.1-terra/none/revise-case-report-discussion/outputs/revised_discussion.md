## Discussion

Euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis (euDKA) is a rare but potentially life-threatening complication of sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitor therapy. These agents promote glycosuria, reduce insulin requirements, and enhance ketogenesis. Studies have reported an up to threefold increase in DKA risk among patients receiving SGLT2 inhibitors. Surgical stress, prolonged fasting, and reduced carbohydrate intake can further predispose patients to ketoacidosis.

In this patient, empagliflozin was continued until the morning before surgery, and she fasted for 14 hours preoperatively. These factors likely contributed to the development of euDKA. The FDA recommends discontinuing empagliflozin at least 3 days before scheduled surgery [5].

Diagnosis was made approximately 14 hours after the first abnormal blood gas. The patient’s initial nausea, vomiting, and abdominal discomfort were attributed to postoperative ileus and opioid analgesia, while her capillary glucose was 182 mg/dL. The absence of marked hyperglycemia can delay recognition of DKA and supports measuring ketones in patients taking SGLT2 inhibitors who present with compatible symptoms. Following diagnosis, she received intravenous insulin with dextrose, fluids, and potassium replacement; the anion gap closed after 38 hours.

## Conclusion

This case illustrates the need to consider euDKA in postoperative patients taking SGLT2 inhibitors, even in the absence of marked hyperglycemia. Perioperative planning should include attention to recommended medication discontinuation, and compatible symptoms should prompt evaluation for ketoacidosis.
