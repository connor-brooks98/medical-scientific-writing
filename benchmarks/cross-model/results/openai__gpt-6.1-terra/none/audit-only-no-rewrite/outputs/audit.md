# Introduction audit

## Main issues

1. **The causal claims go beyond the evidence presented.** “Studies have shown that early norepinephrine improves survival” needs support from appropriately designed studies. More clearly, the institutional retrospective analysis reports an association, but the next sentence says early norepinephrine “prevents death.” Adjustment does not establish causation; differences between patients who received earlier and later treatment may still explain some or all of the association.

2. **The reported effect needs careful interpretation.** The adjusted odds ratio of 0.78 describes lower *odds* of 28-day mortality in the early-treatment group, relative to a comparison group that is not explicitly defined. It is not a risk ratio or an absolute reduction in mortality. The introduction also does not say whether the confidence interval accounts for the analysis’s important limitations.

3. **The retrospective finding raises timing and design questions.** Define “shock onset,” identify the comparator, and clarify how treatment timing was measured. In particular, assess whether patients had to survive long enough to be classified as receiving norepinephrine within one hour, and whether the analysis appropriately handled time-related bias. Confounding by illness severity and treatment decisions is also a concern.

4. **The knowledge gap is asserted, not identified.** “This critical gap” has no clear antecedent. The introduction should establish what remains uncertain despite existing research—for example, a question about timing, outcomes, populations, or study design—and why the proposed study is needed to address it.

5. **The proposed study lacks a concrete purpose.** “A comprehensive prospective study” does not tell the reader its objective, population, comparison, or main outcome. Nor does “prospective” establish whether it is observational or interventional, which matters for what it can conclude.

## Support and context

- The broad claim that sepsis is a leading cause of death worldwide needs a source, as do the claims about the existing early-vasopressor evidence.
- The opening moves from sepsis generally to septic shock specifically without explaining the connection or why vasopressor timing matters in that setting.
- The introduction does not explain why the one-hour threshold or 28-day mortality outcome was chosen.
- Clarify whether the institutional analysis is published, preliminary, or part of the work being introduced, and cite it if appropriate.

## Style and structure

- The first paragraph uses broad, generic language—“pivotal challenge,” “evolving landscape,” and “growing interest”—where a more specific rationale would be more informative.
- The two statements about early treatment largely repeat each other, while shifting from the strong “improves survival” to the heavily qualified “may potentially be associated with worse outcomes in some patients.”
- The final sentence repeats the idea of an “important gap” and overpromises: a study cannot be assumed to fill the gap or improve outcomes before its results are known.
- The transition from the retrospective result to the proposed study is abrupt. The limitations of the prior finding should make clear why the next study is warranted.
