## Discussion

Euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis (euDKA) was diagnosed on postoperative day 2, approximately 14 hours after the first blood gas showed metabolic acidosis. The surgical team initially attributed the patient's nausea, vomiting, and abdominal discomfort to postoperative ileus and opioid analgesia. Her capillary glucose was 182 mg/dL. When tachypnea developed, testing showed a pH of 7.12 and an anion gap of 24 mmol/L. Serum beta-hydroxybutyrate was 6.8 mmol/L.

EuDKA is an uncommon but potentially fatal complication of sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitor therapy [CITATION NEEDED: frequency and severity of euDKA with SGLT2 inhibitors]. In patients receiving these drugs, the reported risk of diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) is up to threefold higher [CITATION NEEDED: risk estimate, comparator, and study population]. SGLT2 inhibitors are used to treat type 2 diabetes, heart failure, and chronic kidney disease. They promote glycosuria, reduce insulin requirements, and favor ketogenesis. Surgical stress, prolonged fasting, and reduced carbohydrate intake can further predispose patients to ketoacidosis. Because marked hyperglycemia may be absent, glucose alone is insufficient to exclude DKA.

The US Food and Drug Administration recommends stopping empagliflozin at least 3 days before scheduled surgery [5]. Our patient took her last dose the morning before surgery and fasted for 14 hours preoperatively. Both factors may have contributed to euDKA, although their individual contributions cannot be determined from this case. The anion gap closed after 38 hours of treatment with intravenous insulin, concurrent 10% dextrose, fluids, and potassium replacement. Empagliflozin was discontinued permanently.

## Conclusion

In postoperative patients taking SGLT2 inhibitors who develop nausea, vomiting, or unexplained tachypnea, clinicians should assess for metabolic acidosis and measure ketones even if glucose is normal or only modestly elevated. Before elective surgery, the perioperative team should confirm when the patient last took empagliflozin and whether the recommended hold interval has been met.
