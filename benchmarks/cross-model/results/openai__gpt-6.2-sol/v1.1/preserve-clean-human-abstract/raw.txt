Importance: Short sleep has been associated with cardiovascular disease, but whether it is associated with incident atrial fibrillation (AF) in older adults is uncertain.

Objective: To examine the association between self-reported sleep duration and incident AF in adults aged 65 years or older.

Design, Setting, and Participants: This prospective cohort study included 4,912 community-dwelling adults without AF at baseline. Participants were enrolled at 4 US sites between 2009 and 2012 and followed up through 2022.

Exposures: Self-reported habitual sleep duration of 6 hours or less, 7 to 8 hours (reference), or 9 hours or more.

Main Outcomes and Measures: Incident AF was ascertained from study electrocardiograms, hospital discharge codes, and Medicare claims.

Results: During a median follow-up of 9.8 years, 781 participants developed AF. Compared with participants who reported 7 to 8 hours of sleep, the adjusted hazard ratio for AF was 1.29 (95% CI, 1.08–1.54) among those reporting 6 hours or less and 1.07 (95% CI, 0.86–1.33) among those reporting 9 hours or more. After further adjustment for symptoms of obstructive sleep apnea, the hazard ratio for 6 hours or less was 1.18 (95% CI, 0.98–1.42).

Conclusions and Relevance: In this cohort of older adults, short sleep was associated with a higher hazard of incident AF, but the association weakened after adjustment for symptoms of obstructive sleep apnea. Whether extending sleep or treating obstructive sleep apnea lowers AF risk is a question for randomized trials.
