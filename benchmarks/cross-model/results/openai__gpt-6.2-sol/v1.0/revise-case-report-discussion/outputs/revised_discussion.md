## Discussion

Euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis (euDKA) was diagnosed on postoperative day (POD) 2, approximately 14 hours after the first blood gas showed acidosis. On POD 1, the surgical team initially attributed the patient's nausea, vomiting, and abdominal discomfort to postoperative ileus and opioid analgesia. Her capillary glucose was 182 mg/dL. When tachypnea developed that evening, testing showed a pH of 7.12, a bicarbonate level of 9 mmol/L, and an anion gap of 24 mmol/L. Serum beta-hydroxybutyrate was 6.8 mmol/L.

EuDKA is an uncommon but potentially fatal complication of sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitor therapy [CITATION NEEDED: frequency and severity of euDKA associated with SGLT2 inhibitors]. SGLT2 inhibitors promote glycosuria, reduce insulin requirements, and favor ketogenesis. Their use has been associated with up to a threefold increase in the risk of diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) [CITATION NEEDED: study, population, and comparator for the threefold risk estimate]. Surgical stress and reduced carbohydrate intake during fasting can further favor ketogenesis. In this patient, a 14-hour preoperative fast and empagliflozin exposure through the morning before surgery may have contributed.

The US Food and Drug Administration recommends stopping empagliflozin at least 3 days before scheduled surgery [5]. In this case, the last dose was taken the morning before surgery. After transfer to intensive care, the patient received intravenous insulin with concurrent 10% dextrose, fluid resuscitation, and potassium replacement. The anion gap closed after 38 hours, and empagliflozin was permanently discontinued.

## Conclusion

In postoperative patients taking an SGLT2 inhibitor, nausea or vomiting accompanied by tachypnea or unexplained anion gap acidosis should prompt serum ketone measurement even when glucose is normal or only modestly elevated. For scheduled surgery, the perioperative medication plan should specify when the patient should take the last dose of empagliflozin.
