## Discussion

Among 1,146 patients with dementia who underwent hip fracture surgery, mobilization within 24 hours was associated with lower 30-day mortality than no mobilization within that window (6.2% vs 9.8%; adjusted odds ratio, 0.61; 95% CI, 0.39–0.95). Median length of stay was 8 vs 11 days, and delirium occurred in 31% vs 29% of patients, respectively. The observed delirium rates alone do not establish whether early mobilization affects delirium risk.

Patients who were well enough to mobilize soon after surgery may also have been more likely to survive and leave hospital sooner. Preoperative ambulatory status was undocumented for 18% of patients; lack of documentation does not establish that they were unable to walk. If preoperative mobility influenced both when patients mobilized and their outcomes, incomplete documentation limits our ability to assess that source of confounding.

These findings do not establish the safety of early mobilization or show whether it was feasible for patients with severe dementia.
