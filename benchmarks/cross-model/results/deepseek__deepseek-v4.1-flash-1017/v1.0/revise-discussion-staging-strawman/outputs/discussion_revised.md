## Discussion

In this retrospective cohort of 1,146 patients with dementia, mobilization within 24 hours of hip fracture surgery was associated with lower 30-day mortality (6.2% vs 9.8%; adjusted odds ratio, 0.61; 95% CI, 0.39–0.95) and with shorter length of stay (median 8 vs 11 days) [DATA NEEDED: IQRs for length of stay, and whether that comparison was adjusted]. Delirium incidence did not differ between groups (31% vs 29%).

The delirium result is the least informative of the three. A null difference does not show that early mobilization leaves cognition untouched; a difference smaller than the one this study could have detected would not have appeared in the data [DATA NEEDED: the delirium difference the study had 80% power to detect].

The mortality association has two limits. Patients who can be mobilized on the first day are plausibly those with less severe dementia and fewer comorbidities. The adjusted model can account only for what was measured [DATA NEEDED: the covariates included]. Preoperative ambulatory status was missing for 18% of patients, so baseline mobility could not be included for the whole cohort. If those patients were more often nonambulatory, were mobilized later, and were more likely to die, the association with mortality is overstated; if their missing status was unrelated to mobilization and to death, the estimate is unaffected.

Feasibility is a separate question from benefit. The proportion of the cohort mobilized within 24 hours, and the reasons mobilization was delayed in the remainder, would show how often early mobilization can be achieved in this population [DATA NEEDED: both figures].

These data are consistent with early mobilization being safe in patients who can be mobilized on the first postoperative day. They do not establish that earlier mobilization is what lowered mortality. A trial that assigned patients to mobilization within 24 hours or usual care, with preoperative mobility and dementia severity recorded at baseline, would answer that question [CITATION NEEDED: any registered or published trial of early mobilization after hip fracture in patients with dementia].
