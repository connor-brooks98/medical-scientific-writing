## Discussion

Euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis (euDKA) is an uncommon complication of sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitor therapy. It is easy to overlook after surgery: the glucose does not suggest ketoacidosis, and nausea and vomiting have ordinary explanations. Our patient's capillary glucose was 182 mg/dL on POD 1, when her symptoms were attributed to ileus and opioid analgesia, and her venous pH was 7.12, her bicarbonate 9 mmol/L, her anion gap 24 mmol/L, and her beta-hydroxybutyrate 6.8 mmol/L. The diagnosis was recorded on the morning of POD 2, about 14 hours after the first abnormal gas.

SGLT2 inhibitors are prescribed for type 2 diabetes, heart failure, and chronic kidney disease. They lower glucose by promoting glycosuria, which reduces the insulin requirement and increases ketogenesis. Surgical stress and a long fast add to ketone production; our patient fasted for 14 hours before an operation that was otherwise uncomplicated. The reported risk of diabetic ketoacidosis is up to three-fold higher in patients taking these drugs [CITATION NEEDED: source of the three-fold estimate].

The FDA recommends stopping empagliflozin at least 3 days before scheduled surgery [5]. Our patient took her last dose on the morning before surgery. Metformin was held that morning; empagliflozin was not.

With an insulin infusion, concurrent 10% dextrose, fluid resuscitation, and potassium replacement, the anion gap closed after 38 hours and she was discharged on POD 6. The timing is consistent with empagliflozin contributing to the episode, but one patient cannot establish it. Nor can this case show whether stopping the drug 3 days before surgery would have prevented the ketoacidosis, or how much the 14 hours between the first abnormal gas and the diagnosis affected her course. Only the single capillary glucose of 182 mg/dL is recorded, so the trajectory that preceded the acidosis is unavailable.

## Conclusion

In this patient, euDKA followed an elective laparoscopic cholecystectomy, with empagliflozin continued until the morning before surgery. A glucose of 182 mg/dL did not exclude it. In a patient taking an SGLT2 inhibitor, postoperative nausea and vomiting warrant measurement of beta-hydroxybutyrate rather than an assumption of ileus, and elective surgery should be scheduled at least 3 days after the last dose [5]. Whether ketone screening on POD 1 detects euDKA earlier in patients whose SGLT2 inhibitor was not stopped in time would require a prospective cohort study.
