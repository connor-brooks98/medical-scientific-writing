Importance: Short sleep has been associated with cardiovascular disease, but its association with incident atrial fibrillation (AF) in older adults is uncertain.

Objective: To examine whether self-reported sleep duration is associated with incident AF in adults aged 65 years or older.

Design, Setting, and Participants: This prospective cohort study included 4,912 community-dwelling adults aged 65 years or older without AF at baseline, enrolled at 4 US sites between 2009 and 2012 and followed up through 2022.

Exposure: Self-reported habitual sleep duration, categorized as 6 hours or less, 7 to 8 hours (reference), and 9 hours or more.

Main Outcomes and Measures: Incident AF, ascertained from study electrocardiograms, hospital discharge codes, and Medicare claims.

Results: During a median follow-up of 9.8 years, 781 participants developed AF. Compared with 7 to 8 hours, sleeping 6 hours or less was associated with a higher hazard of AF (adjusted hazard ratio, 1.29; 95% CI, 1.08–1.54), whereas sleeping 9 hours or more was not (adjusted hazard ratio, 1.07; 95% CI, 0.86–1.33). The association for short sleep was attenuated after further adjustment for obstructive sleep apnea symptoms (adjusted hazard ratio, 1.18; 95% CI, 0.98–1.42).

Conclusions and Relevance: In this cohort of older adults, short sleep was associated with incident AF, but the association was weaker after adjustment for symptoms of sleep apnea. Whether treating sleep apnea or extending sleep lowers AF risk would require trials.
<<<END FILE>>>

There was very little to fix. Your abstract has no em dashes, no "furthermore/notably" openers, no -ing interpretation tails, no stated moral, no "more research is needed" coda, and no evaluative adjectives. Every sentence carries a number or a decision, which is what actually distinguishes expert abstract prose from generated prose. I made four small edits and left the rest alone.

**What changed**
- **Structure:** nothing removed. Your null long-sleep result and the attenuated short-sleep estimate both stay where you put them, at the same prominence.
- **Sentences:** the Design sentence had a chained relative clause ("...adults ... who were enrolled ... and followed up ..."). It is now a participial phrase, and "used data from" became "included."
- **Words:** "relationship with" → "association with" (one term per concept, since the rest of the abstract uses "associated with"); "after adjustment for" → "after further adjustment for" (makes clear the sleep apnea adjustment sits on top of the primary model, which is what the drop from 1.29 to 1.18 implies); "after accounting for" → "after adjustment for" for the same consistency reason.
- **Added** "aged 65 years or older" in Design, taken from your Objective, so each structured field stands alone.

**Fidelity:** all numbers, CIs, dates, site count, and follow-up duration are unchanged (4,912; 4 sites; 2009, 2012, 2022; 9.8 years; 781; 1.29/1.07/1.18 with their CIs). The only addition is the age criterion, already stated in your Objective. The revision is slightly shorter than the original.

**Author queries**
1. You report relative hazards only. JAMA Cardiology reviewers often ask for an absolute measure (events per 1,000 person-years, or cumulative incidence by sleep category). If you have it, one clause in Results would strengthen the abstract.
2. "Would require trials" is fine and specific enough to keep; "a randomized trial" would be sharper if you prefer.
3. Check the submission template for two things I did not change: whether JAMA uses "Exposures" (plural) as the field heading, and whether it sets CI ranges with hyphens rather than en dashes.
4. I can't promise anything about AI detectors, and you shouldn't count on that. If you use this edited version, check JAMA Cardiology's policy: ICMJE asks authors to disclose AI-assisted writing, though spelling and grammar tools are generally exempt, so judge where a line edit falls.

One honest note: the sentence a skeptical reviewer might press on is not style but substance. "Short sleep was associated with incident AF" in the conclusion sits alongside a confidence interval that crosses 1 after sleep apnea adjustment. Your wording already flags the attenuation, which is the right call; just be ready to say in the response to reviewers that the primary model is the prespecified one.
