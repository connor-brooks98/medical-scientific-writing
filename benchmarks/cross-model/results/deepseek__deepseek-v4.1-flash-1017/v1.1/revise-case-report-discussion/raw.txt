## Discussion

Euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis (euDKA) is an uncommon and potentially life-threatening complication of sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitor therapy [CITATION NEEDED: incidence and reported mortality]. In our patient it developed on the first day after elective laparoscopic cholecystectomy.

SGLT2 inhibitors, prescribed for type 2 diabetes, heart failure, and chronic kidney disease, lower glucose by promoting glycosuria. The same mechanism reduces insulin requirements and favors ketogenesis, and surgical stress, fasting, and reduced carbohydrate intake add to it. Our patient took her last dose of empagliflozin 10 mg on the morning before surgery and fasted for 14 hours preoperatively.

The risk of DKA is reported to be up to three-fold higher in patients taking these drugs [CITATION NEEDED: the study, its comparator, and the population studied]. Because glucose is often only modestly elevated, the presentation can be mistaken for a routine postoperative problem. In our patient, nausea, vomiting, and abdominal discomfort on POD 1 were attributed to ileus and opioid analgesia, and her capillary glucose of 182 mg/dL gave no reason to suspect ketoacidosis. A venous blood gas that evening showed pH 7.12, bicarbonate 9 mmol/L, and an anion gap of 24 mmol/L; lactate was 1.4 mmol/L. Serum beta-hydroxybutyrate was 6.8 mmol/L, and the diagnosis was made the next morning, about 14 hours after the first abnormal gas.

Treatment was intravenous insulin with concurrent 10% dextrose, fluid resuscitation, and potassium replacement. The anion gap closed after 38 hours, empagliflozin was stopped permanently, and she was discharged on POD 6 taking metformin and basal insulin.

The FDA label for empagliflozin recommends stopping it at least 3 days before scheduled surgery [5]. Continuing the drug until the day before surgery likely contributed. One case cannot show whether measuring ketones when the first symptoms appeared would have led to earlier treatment, or how much the 14-hour fast added.

## Conclusion

In this patient, euDKA developed on the first day after laparoscopic cholecystectomy, with empagliflozin continued until the morning before surgery and a 14-hour preoperative fast. Her capillary glucose on POD 1 was 182 mg/dL, and the diagnosis was made about 14 hours after the first abnormal blood gas. Postoperative nausea, vomiting, malaise, or abdominal pain in a patient taking an SGLT2 inhibitor warrants measurement of beta-hydroxybutyrate regardless of the glucose value. Empagliflozin should be stopped at least 3 days before elective surgery, as the label recommends [5].
