## Discussion

In this retrospective cohort of 1,146 patients with dementia, mobilization within 24 hours of hip fracture surgery was associated with lower 30-day mortality (6.2% vs 9.8%; adjusted odds ratio, 0.61; 95% CI, 0.39–0.95) and with shorter length of stay (median 8 vs 11 days) [DATA NEEDED: adjusted estimate and 95% CI for length of stay].

Delirium incidence did not differ between groups (31% vs 29%) [DATA NEEDED: 95% CI or P value for the difference]. Whether early mobilization affects cognition is therefore still open.

Mobilization timing is a bedside decision, so the patients mobilized later are likely to include those who were more ill at baseline. Adjustment accounted for the confounders that were measured, not for all of them, and this cohort cannot show that mobilizing earlier would itself have lowered mortality. Preoperative ambulatory status was missing for 18% of patients [DATA NEEDED: how missing status was handled in the adjusted model], and early mobilization was achieved in most patients [DATA NEEDED: proportion mobilized within 24 hours, and the recorded reasons for delay].

A trial assigning patients to mobilization within 24 hours or to usual timing, with mortality and return to ambulation as outcomes, would separate the timing of mobilization from the severity of the patient's condition.
