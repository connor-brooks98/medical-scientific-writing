Introduction

Atrial fibrillation (AF) is the most common sustained cardiac arrhythmia, and its prevalence rises with age. Adults aged 75 years and older have the highest prevalence and the highest stroke risk [CITATION NEEDED: age-specific prevalence and stroke risk in this age group]. Much of this AF is paroxysmal and asymptomatic, so clinic-based ECG recording misses it and detection requires prolonged monitoring [CITATION NEEDED: proportion of AF that is paroxysmal or asymptomatic]. Whether detecting AF earlier prevents stroke is being tested in randomized screening trials [CITATION NEEDED: the relevant trials, with their stroke outcomes].

Consumer smartwatches record photoplethysmography (PPG) traces and, in some models, single-lead electrocardiograms (ECG); the device algorithm flags an irregular rhythm for review. Validation studies have reported high sensitivity and specificity, but they enrolled mainly younger, community-dwelling adults [CITATION NEEDED: named validation studies, with population, reference standard, sensitivity, and specificity]. Signal quality may be worse in the oldest patients. Tremor, peripheral vascular disease, and frequent ectopy are common in this group and degrade both PPG and single-lead ECG recordings [CITATION NEEDED: effect of these conditions on signal quality].
