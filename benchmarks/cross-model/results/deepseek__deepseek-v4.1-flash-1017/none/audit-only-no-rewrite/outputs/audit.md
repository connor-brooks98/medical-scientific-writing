# Audit of `introduction.txt`

## Overall assessment
The introduction has a sensible broad topic, but it currently moves too quickly from general importance to unsupported literature claims, then to a single retrospective institutional result, then to a causal conclusion and a vaguely defined “critical gap.” The main problems are evidentiary overreach, undefined key terms, internal inconsistency in the stated gap, missing citations, and promotional/future-tense claims about the proposed study.

No references are included, so the factual and literature claims cannot be verified from the provided text.

## Major issues

1. **Literature claims are not cited or specified.**
   - “Sepsis remains a leading cause of death worldwide” needs a source and a defined scope.
   - “Studies have shown that early norepinephrine improves survival in septic shock” does not identify the studies, their designs, populations, timing definitions, or effect sizes.
   - “A growing body of evidence” is vague and unsupported without citations.
   - The retrospective institutional analysis is described without a citation, registration, or clear statement of whether it is preliminary/unpublished data.

2. **Causal overreach.**
   - The statement “Early norepinephrine therefore prevents death in septic shock” is too strong for a retrospective association.
   - An adjusted odds ratio of 0.78 suggests an association, but retrospective observational data are vulnerable to confounding by indication, immortal time bias, severity confounding, residual confounding, and timing-definition problems.
   - An odds ratio is not a direct risk difference and does not by itself establish that the exposure “prevents death.”

3. **Key terms are undefined or used inconsistently.**
   - “Early” is not defined in the literature summary; the institutional analysis uses “within 1 hour,” but the introduction does not reconcile these.
   - “Shock onset” is not defined.
   - “Sepsis” and “septic shock” are used somewhat interchangeably even though they are different populations.
   - “Delayed vasopressor therapy” is not defined relative to a comparator or time zero.
   - “Outcomes” is vague in the sentence about delayed therapy.

4. **The evidence gap is not established.**
   - The paragraph first says studies have shown that early norepinephrine improves survival, then later says there is a “critical gap” without explaining what remains unknown.
   - It is unclear whether the gap is about prospective confirmation, residual confounding, timing thresholds, generalizability, specific subgroups, or something else.
   - “To address this critical gap” appears abruptly and is not logically prepared by the preceding sentences.

5. **The proposed study is described with promotional language rather than scientific specificity.**
   - “Comprehensive prospective study” is subjective and does not tell the reader the design, population, exposure, comparator, or outcomes.
   - “Will not only fill an important gap in the literature but also pave the way for improved outcomes” overpromises impact before results are known.
   - The final sentence sounds like a grant pitch rather than a precise introduction to a study objective or hypothesis.

6. **Internal logic and flow are weak.**
   - Paragraph 1 moves from global burden to vague interest to broad evidence.
   - Paragraph 2 presents a single retrospective analysis, immediately converts it into a causal claim, then jumps to an unspecified gap and a proposed prospective study.
   - The transition from “studies have shown benefit” to “we designed a study to fill a gap” needs an explicit unresolved question.

7. **Reporting of the retrospective analysis is insufficient for the claim being made.**
   - The analysis reports n, exposure timing, outcome, adjusted odds ratio, and confidence interval, but not the adjustment variables, model, shock definition, inclusion criteria, or how timing was ascertained.
   - If this is preliminary data motivating the study, it should be clearly signposted as such.
   - If it is prior published work, it needs a citation.

8. **Tone and style issues.**
   - “Pivotal challenge in the evolving landscape of critical care” is vague and clichéd.
   - “In recent years, there has been growing interest” is filler unless tied to specific evidence.
   - “May potentially be associated” is redundant hedging.
   - “Some patients” is vague in the delayed-therapy sentence.
   - The prose sometimes combines multiple claims in one sentence, reducing clarity.

## Sentence-level audit notes
- “Sepsis remains a leading cause of death worldwide…” — needs citation and a clear sepsis/septic shock scope.
- “…representing a pivotal challenge in the evolving landscape of critical care.” — vague and promotional.
- “In recent years, there has been growing interest…” — vague timeframe and no citation.
- “Studies have shown that early norepinephrine improves survival in septic shock.” — causal wording may overstate the evidence; “early” is undefined; studies are not cited.
- “Furthermore, a growing body of evidence suggests that delayed vasopressor therapy may potentially be associated with worse outcomes in some patients.” — redundant hedging, vague “some patients,” undefined “delayed,” and no citation.
- “In a retrospective analysis of 2,914 patients with septic shock at our institution…” — unclear whether this is preliminary, unpublished, or previously published; lacks citation/context.
- “…norepinephrine started within 1 hour of shock onset was associated with lower 28-day mortality…” — association is appropriately worded here, but “shock onset” and adjustment strategy are undefined.
- “Early norepinephrine therefore prevents death in septic shock.” — major causal overstatement and non sequitur from the preceding retrospective association.
- “To address this critical gap…” — the gap has not been specified.
- “…we designed a comprehensive prospective study…” — “comprehensive” is subjective; design details absent.
- “…that will not only fill an important gap in the literature but also pave the way for improved outcomes.” — overpromises future impact and uses clichéd framing.

## Revision checklist
- Add citations for epidemiologic and literature claims.
- Define sepsis/septic shock, early/delayed exposure, shock onset, comparator, and outcomes.
- Replace causal language with association language unless causal evidence is explicitly cited.
- State the remaining evidence gap precisely.
- Clarify the status and context of the institutional retrospective analysis.
- Remove or temper promotional claims about the proposed study’s future impact.
- Reduce vague hedging and generic filler.
- End with a clear study objective or hypothesis rather than a broad promise.
