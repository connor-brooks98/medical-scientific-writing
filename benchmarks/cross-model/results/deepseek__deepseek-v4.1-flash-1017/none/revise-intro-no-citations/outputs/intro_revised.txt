Introduction

Atrial fibrillation (AF) is the most common sustained cardiac arrhythmia and is associated with substantial morbidity, mortality, and health-care burden, with risk increasing markedly with age [1–3]. Smartwatches equipped with photoplethysmography (PPG) and/or single-lead electrocardiography (ECG) have emerged as promising tools for opportunistic and ambulatory AF screening [4–6]. Algorithmic detection of AF by these devices has shown high sensitivity and specificity in selected and general adult populations [7–9], and early detection may enable timely anticoagulation and reduce AF-related stroke, although outcome data from wearable-led screening are still evolving [10–12].

Older adults bear the greatest burden of AF and AF-related stroke, yet they remain underrepresented in validation studies of smartwatch-based AF detection. This is a critical gap because age-related and comorbid conditions—such as tremor, frequent ectopy, peripheral vascular disease, and potential limitations in device use—may impair signal quality and algorithmic performance. Diagnostic accuracy observed in younger or general adult cohorts therefore cannot be assumed to generalize to adults aged 75 years or older, highlighting the need for population-specific validation.

We therefore conducted a prospective diagnostic accuracy study to evaluate a commercially available smartwatch for detecting AF in adults aged ≥75 years, using 14-day continuous ambulatory ECG patch monitoring as the reference standard. By focusing on this high-risk and understudied population, this study aims to provide clinically relevant evidence to inform AF screening and device use in older adults.
