## Discussion

Euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis (euDKA) is an uncommon but serious complication of sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitor therapy. SGLT2 inhibitors lower glucose by promoting urinary glucose excretion and can also reduce insulin requirements and promote ketogenesis. In the perioperative period, surgical stress, fasting, and reduced carbohydrate intake may unmask or worsen this tendency, predisposing patients to ketoacidosis without marked hyperglycemia. The risk of diabetic ketoacidosis has been reported to be increased up to threefold in patients receiving SGLT2 inhibitors. Because glucose may be only modestly elevated or near normal, diagnosis can be delayed unless ketones are measured in patients who present with nausea, vomiting, malaise, or other symptoms compatible with ketoacidosis.

In this patient, empagliflozin was continued until the morning before surgery, and the 14-hour preoperative fast may have further reduced carbohydrate availability. Postoperatively, nausea, vomiting, and abdominal discomfort were initially attributed to ileus and opioid analgesia. Her capillary glucose was 182 mg/dL, a value that did not suggest diabetic ketoacidosis. However, tachypnea on the evening of postoperative day 1 prompted a venous blood gas, which showed pH 7.12, bicarbonate 9 mmol/L, and an anion gap of 24 mmol/L. Lactate was 1.4 mmol/L and beta-hydroxybutyrate was 6.8 mmol/L, confirming euDKA. The diagnosis was made on the morning of postoperative day 2, approximately 14 hours after the first abnormal blood gas. This sequence illustrates the diagnostic challenge posed by euDKA: nonspecific postoperative symptoms and the absence of marked hyperglycemia may divert attention from ketoacidosis, and ketone measurement is required to establish the diagnosis.

The FDA recommends that empagliflozin be discontinued at least 3 days before scheduled surgery [5]. In our patient, the drug was continued until the day before surgery, which likely contributed to the development of euDKA. Once the diagnosis was established, treatment with intravenous insulin infusion and concurrent 10% dextrose, fluid resuscitation, and potassium replacement resulted in closure of the anion gap after 38 hours. Empagliflozin was discontinued permanently, and the patient was discharged on postoperative day 6 on metformin and basal insulin. This case supports the need for perioperative protocols that include preoperative SGLT2 inhibitor discontinuation and postoperative ketone testing when symptoms suggestive of ketoacidosis occur.

## Conclusion

This case demonstrates euDKA after laparoscopic cholecystectomy in a patient whose empagliflozin was continued until the day before surgery. Although the patient's glucose was only 182 mg/dL at the time symptoms developed, marked ketoacidosis was present. Clinicians should consider euDKA in postoperative patients receiving SGLT2 inhibitors who develop nausea, vomiting, abdominal discomfort, or tachypnea, even when glucose is not significantly elevated. Adherence to recommended preoperative discontinuation of SGLT2 inhibitors and prompt ketone testing are essential to reduce the risk of delayed diagnosis and severe metabolic complications.
