Here's what I think was reading off, and what I changed:

**1. Causal language on observational data.** "This illustrates the importance of early mobilization" treated an adjusted odds ratio as proof of effect. Worse, the design has two specific biases the original never named: *confounding by indication/reverse causation* (patients who can mobilize at 24 hours are the ones already recovering well) and *immortal time* (you must survive the first 24 hours to be counted as "early," so early deaths land in the delayed group by construction). Either could generate the mortality finding on its own. I added both, plus death as a competing event for length of stay.

**2. The missing-data inference was backwards.** "Not recorded for 18%... suggesting that these patients were likely nonambulatory" converts missingness into a clinical finding. Documentation gaps track admission route, transfers, and dementia severity — not function. I reframed it as missing information and suggested imputation plus best/worst-case sensitivity analyses.

**3. The delirium reasoning contradicted itself.** "A lack of difference does not mean no cognitive effects. It means our study could not detect them" asserts undetected effects exist, which the data don't show either. The honest statement is that the comparison is inconclusive: no equivalence margin, no reported precision, chart-based ascertainment that underdetects hypoactive delirium against a dementia baseline.

**4. The severe-dementia rebuttal didn't follow.** A null delirium result says nothing about whether patients with severe dementia can participate in physiotherapy. That objection needs severity stratification and feasibility data (attempted vs. achieved, assistance level, reasons for deferral), none of which are reported — so "feasible for most patients" is currently unsupported.

**5. The safety/benefit switch.** The opening asks about safety, but mortality and length of stay are benefit outcomes. I flagged that no adverse-event data are presented, so safety remains unestablished.
