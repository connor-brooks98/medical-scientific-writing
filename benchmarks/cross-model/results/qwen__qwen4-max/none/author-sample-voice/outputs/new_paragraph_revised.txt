In this cluster-randomized trial across 12 hospitals, pharmacist-led medication reconciliation at discharge reduced adverse drug events within 30 days from 14.2% to 10.6% (risk difference, -3.6 percentage points; 95% CI, -5.9 to -1.3). Two features of the result deserve comment. First, the absolute difference is modest — about one event avoided for every 28 patients discharged — and the interval does not exclude a benefit as small as 1.3 percentage points. Whether that justifies the pharmacy time it requires is a question our trial does not answer. Second, the effect was larger in patients taking 10 or more medications (risk difference, -6.1 percentage points). Patients on more drugs have more discrepancies to find, so a larger effect is plausible; but they also start at higher risk, and we cannot separate the two explanations. We would treat this subgroup as a hypothesis to be tested rather than a basis for targeting.
