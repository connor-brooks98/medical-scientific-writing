Honest read first: this doesn't sound AI-written. It sounds like a competent epidemiology abstract. The usual tells — "underscores," "highlights the importance," "robust," "delve," vague hedging, triadic lists, a punchy summary sentence that restates everything — aren't here. Your sentences are short, the numbers are specific, and the conclusion is appropriately deflationary. If a detector flags this, it's flagging the structured-abstract genre, not your writing. I'd also gently note that AI detectors are unreliable in both directions, so I can't promise any edit will change a score, and reviewers at JAMA Cardiology are far more likely to scrutinize your methods than your prose style.

So I made only small changes, and none of them alter a number or a claim:

- Importance: "its relationship with... is uncertain" → "whether it is associated with... is unclear." Slightly more direct.
- Design: "used data from... who were enrolled" → "included... enrolled." Removes a layer of scaffolding.
- Results: recast the comparison so participants are the subject ("Participants reporting 6 hours or less had a higher hazard...") rather than the gerund construction "sleeping 6 hours or less was associated with." This is the one edit I'd defend on style grounds — it's more concrete and reads more like a person describing their cohort.
- "The association for short sleep was attenuated after adjustment for" → "Adjustment for obstructive sleep apnea symptoms attenuated the short-sleep estimate." Active, tighter.
- Conclusion: "the association was weaker" → "attenuated," and "Whether... would require trials" → "Trials would be needed to determine whether..." The original last sentence had an awkward conditional structure.

Two things that matter more than the AI question:

1. **The file has a bracketed placeholder — don't submit it as-is.** JAMA structured abstracts require participant characteristics at the start of Results: mean (SD) age, sex distribution, and ideally the n in each sleep category. Yours jumps straight to follow-up time. I left a marker rather than inventing values. Consider also adding incidence rates per 1000 person-years by category; reviewers like an absolute number alongside the hazard ratios.

2. **Expect a reviewer question about the OSA adjustment.** You describe attenuation after adjusting for OSA symptoms, but if OSA lies on the causal path from short sleep to AF, that's mediation, not confounding, and the adjusted estimate answers a different question than the primary one. Be explicit in the manuscript about which you're treating it as. Related: "symptoms" is not diagnosed OSA, and a reader will want that limitation stated plainly. Also worth pre-empting: the primary CI (1.08–1.54) is not far from null, so avoid language elsewhere in the paper that oversells it.
