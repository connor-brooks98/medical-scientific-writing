## Discussion

In this retrospective cohort of 1,146 patients with dementia, mobilization within 24 hours of hip fracture surgery was associated with lower 30-day mortality (6.2% vs 9.8%; adjusted odds ratio, 0.61; 95% CI, 0.39–0.95) and shorter length of stay (median 8 vs 11 days). Delirium incidence did not differ between groups (31% vs 29%) [DATA NEEDED: adjusted estimate with 95% CI for the delirium comparison, and how delirium was ascertained]. That result does not exclude a smaller difference in delirium, or an effect on cognition that delirium incidence would not capture.

These estimates are open to confounding by indication: patients mobilized within 24 hours are likely to be those well enough to mobilize, and adjustment cannot fully remove that difference. Preoperative ambulatory status was not recorded for 18% of patients [DATA NEEDED: how missing ambulatory status was handled in the adjusted model]. Those 18% cannot be assumed to have been nonambulatory before the fracture, so confounding by baseline function remains.
