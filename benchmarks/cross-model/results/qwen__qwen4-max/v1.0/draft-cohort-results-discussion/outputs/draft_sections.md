# Draft sections: home PPI use at admission and 90-day *C difficile* infection in older adults hospitalized with community-acquired pneumonia

## Results

We excluded 312 patients (5.0%) because medication reconciliation data were missing, leaving 6214 adults aged 65 years or older admitted with community-acquired pneumonia between 2016 and 2023; 2377 were using a proton pump inhibitor (PPI) at admission and 3837 were not. *Clostridioides difficile* infection (CDI) within 90 days of discharge occurred in 4.1% of PPI users and 2.6% of nonusers [DATA NEEDED: number of CDI events in each group and median follow-up time]. In a Cox model adjusted for age, sex, Charlson comorbidity index, hospitalization in the previous 12 months, and antibiotic class during the index admission, PPI use at admission was associated with CDI (adjusted hazard ratio [aHR], 1.48; 95% CI, 1.12-1.96) (Table 2). Neither secondary outcome was associated with PPI use. All-cause mortality at 90 days was 11.8% in PPI users and 11.2% in nonusers (aHR, 1.03; 95% CI, 0.88-1.21), and readmission at 30 days was 17.9% and 16.4%, respectively (aHR, 1.06; 95% CI, 0.94-1.20). In the prespecified subgroup analysis, the aHR for CDI was 1.81 (95% CI, 1.24-2.64) in men and 1.21 (95% CI, 0.81-1.80) in women, with no evidence of effect modification by sex (P for interaction = .14).

## Discussion

Among 6214 older adults hospitalized with community-acquired pneumonia at a single academic health system, home PPI use at admission was associated with CDI within 90 days of discharge (aHR, 1.48; 95% CI, 1.12-1.96). The absolute difference was 1.5 percentage points, or about 15 additional CDI cases per 1000 patients. Mortality and readmission were not associated with PPI use, although the upper confidence limits (1.21 and 1.20) remain compatible with a relative increase of up to about 20% in either outcome. These data describe an association with an infection outcome, not a benefit from stopping the drug: whether deprescribing a PPI at admission lowers CDI risk would require a randomized trial in this population with CDI as a prespecified outcome.

Our estimate points in the same direction as the meta-analysis by Kwok et al,[1] which reported an association between PPI use and CDI (pooled OR, approximately 1.74) [CITATION NEEDED: exact pooled estimate with 95% CI, and the settings and populations pooled]. Freedberg et al[2] reported an association between PPI use and recurrent CDI; our outcome definition did not separate a first episode from a recurrence [DATA NEEDED: whether CDI history before the index admission was available], so part of what we observed may be recurrence in patients already predisposed to it. One proposed mechanism is that reduced gastric acidity allows ingested organisms to reach the colon, with PPI-associated changes in gut microbiota lowering colonization resistance; we measured neither gastric acidity nor microbiota composition, so this is an explanation we cannot test. Residual confounding is an alternative explanation. PPI users may differ in the indication for the drug, in antibiotic duration and spectrum beyond the class we adjusted for, and in how often they contact our system, which also governs how often they are tested for CDI.

## Limitations

PPI exposure was recorded once, at admission, from medication reconciliation; we had no information on duration, dose, adherence, or whether the drug was continued in hospital. Patients classified as nonusers may have started a PPI during or after the index stay, and long-term users were indistinguishable from recent starters, so we could not examine dose or duration response. CDI was ascertained from inpatient and outpatient laboratory results within our health system only, and testing was ordered at clinician discretion rather than by protocol; infections diagnosed elsewhere were missed, and if PPI users had more frequent contact with our system, they were also more likely to be tested, which would inflate the observed association. We adjusted for antibiotic class but not for duration or spectrum, and we did not account for the indication for the PPI, so confounding by severity of illness and by antibiotic exposure may persist. The 312 patients excluded for missing medication reconciliation may differ from included patients in both PPI use and infection risk. Follow-up ended at 90 days, and the cohort came from one academic system, where CDI testing practice and case mix may differ from community hospitals [DATA NEEDED: whether the CDI testing algorithm changed during 2016-2023].
