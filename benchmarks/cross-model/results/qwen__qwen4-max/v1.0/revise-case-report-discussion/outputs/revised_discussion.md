## Discussion

Euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis (euDKA) is a reported complication of sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitor therapy [CITATION NEEDED: incidence estimate or case series, with source]. These agents lower glucose through urinary glucose loss; the accompanying fall in insulin and rise in glucagon favor ketogenesis, so ketones accumulate while glucose stays near normal. An up to three-fold increase in DKA risk has been reported in patients taking SGLT2 inhibitors [CITATION NEEDED: study design, population, and effect estimate with 95% CI for the three-fold figure]. Fasting and surgical stress act on the same pathway, which is why the perioperative period is a recognized setting for these events [CITATION NEEDED: perioperative precipitating factors].

The delay in our patient came from two plausible explanations. Nausea, vomiting, and abdominal discomfort on postoperative day (POD) 1 were attributed to postoperative ileus and opioid analgesia, both expected after abdominal surgery. A blood gas obtained after she became tachypneic (respiratory rate 28/min) showed pH 7.12, bicarbonate 9 mmol/L, and an anion gap of 24 mmol/L; lactate was 1.4 mmol/L and did not account for the gap. Serum beta-hydroxybutyrate was 6.8 mmol/L, and euDKA was diagnosed on the morning of POD 2, approximately 14 hours after that first gas. The capillary glucose of 182 mg/dL was reassuring and misleading. In a patient taking an SGLT2 inhibitor who vomits after surgery, we would measure beta-hydroxybutyrate and a blood gas at the first assessment rather than waiting for hyperglycemia, and we would treat unexplained tachypnea as a reason to do the same.

The FDA recommends discontinuing empagliflozin at least 3 days before scheduled surgery [5]. Our patient took her last dose on the morning before surgery and then fasted for 14 hours, so the drug effect and the carbohydrate restriction overlapped across the operation and the first postoperative day. Whether the recommended 3-day interval prevents euDKA is not established [CITATION NEEDED: reports of euDKA despite guideline-concordant preoperative interruption, or studies testing the interval itself].

The anion gap closed after 38 hours of treatment with intravenous insulin and concurrent dextrose, and empagliflozin was discontinued permanently. A single case cannot separate the contribution of empagliflozin from that of fasting and surgical stress, and we cannot tell whether recognizing the acidosis 14 hours earlier would have shortened her 38-hour course.

## Conclusion

This patient developed ketoacidosis on the first day after elective laparoscopic cholecystectomy while taking empagliflozin, with a capillary glucose of 182 mg/dL and a serum beta-hydroxybutyrate of 6.8 mmol/L. Her symptoms were first attributed to ileus and opioid analgesia, and the diagnosis followed the first abnormal blood gas by 14 hours. In postoperative patients taking an SGLT2 inhibitor, nausea, vomiting, or unexplained tachypnea should prompt ketone measurement and a blood gas regardless of the glucose value, and empagliflozin should be stopped at least 3 days before scheduled surgery [5].
