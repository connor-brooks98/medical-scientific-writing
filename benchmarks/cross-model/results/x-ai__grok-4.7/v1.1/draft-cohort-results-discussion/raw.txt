## Results

Among 6,214 adults aged 65 years or older admitted with community-acquired pneumonia, 2,377 were using a proton pump inhibitor (PPI) at admission and 3,837 were not. *Clostridioides difficile* infection (CDI) within 90 days of discharge occurred in 4.1% of PPI users and 2.6% of non-users. In a Cox model adjusted for age, sex, Charlson index, hospitalization in the prior 12 months, and antibiotic class during the index stay, PPI use was associated with a higher hazard of CDI (adjusted hazard ratio [HR], 1.48 [95% CI, 1.12–1.96]). Ninety-day all-cause mortality was 11.8% in PPI users and 11.2% in non-users (adjusted HR, 1.03 [95% CI, 0.88–1.21]). Thirty-day readmission was 17.9% in PPI users and 16.4% in non-users (adjusted HR, 1.06 [95% CI, 0.94–1.20]). In prespecified subgroup analyses, the adjusted HR for CDI was 1.81 (95% CI, 1.24–2.64) in men and 1.21 (95% CI, 0.81–1.80) in women (interaction P = .14).

## Discussion

In this cohort of 6,214 adults aged 65 years or older admitted with community-acquired pneumonia in one academic health system, PPI use at admission was associated with a higher hazard of CDI in the 90 days after discharge (adjusted HR, 1.48 [95% CI, 1.12–1.96]). That exposure was not associated with 90-day all-cause mortality or with 30-day readmission; the prespecified interaction with sex was not statistically significant (P = .14).

Kwok et al reported a pooled odds ratio of approximately 1.74 for the association of PPI use with CDI in a meta-analysis.¹ The estimate in this cohort is an adjusted hazard ratio of 1.48 for PPI use recorded at admission among adults aged 65 years or older admitted with community-acquired pneumonia. Freedberg et al reported an association between PPI use and recurrent CDI.² The outcome in this study was CDI detected within 90 days after discharge, not recurrent CDI.

## Limitations

PPI use was defined from the admission medication reconciliation only. Duration, adherence, and use after discharge were unknown; if that misclassification was independent of CDI, the hazard ratio would be biased toward 1. CDI was ascertained from inpatient and outpatient tests in this health system only, so infections diagnosed elsewhere were missed. We do not know whether that under-ascertainment differed by PPI use. We excluded 312 patients (5.0%) for missing medication reconciliation, and the cohort comes from one academic health system in 2016–2023. Residual confounding, including confounding by indication for PPI therapy, is possible, and its direction cannot be determined from these data. Antibiotics were represented by class during the index stay, not by duration and not by antibiotics dispensed after discharge.
