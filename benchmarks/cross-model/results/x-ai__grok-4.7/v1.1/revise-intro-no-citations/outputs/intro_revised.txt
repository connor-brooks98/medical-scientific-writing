Introduction

Studies have reported the sensitivity and specificity of atrial fibrillation (AF) detection by smartwatches equipped with photoplethysmography (PPG) and single-lead electrocardiography (ECG) [CITATION NEEDED: for each study, the signal used, design, age range, and estimates]. The burden of AF is greatest in adults aged 75 years and older [CITATION NEEDED: age-specific prevalence], yet smartwatch accuracy in this age group is uncertain.

Adults aged 75 years and older often have tremor, peripheral vascular disease, and frequent ectopy, which may affect the quality of PPG and single-lead ECG signals [CITATION NEEDED: prevalence of each factor after age 75, and evidence of an effect on wearable signal quality]. A diagnosis of AF often changes the anticoagulation decision at this age [CITATION NEEDED: stroke-risk guideline or score threshold]. A false-positive reading may then lead to monitoring or treatment that would not otherwise be started; a false-negative reading may leave AF undetected.

We conducted a prospective diagnostic accuracy study of a commercially available smartwatch for detecting AF in adults aged 75 years and older. The reference standard was 14-day continuous patch monitoring.
