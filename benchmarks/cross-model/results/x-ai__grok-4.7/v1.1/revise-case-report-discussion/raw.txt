## Discussion

Our patient developed euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis (euDKA) after an uncomplicated laparoscopic cholecystectomy. She had taken her last dose of empagliflozin, 10 mg, on the morning before surgery and had fasted for 14 hours preoperatively. The FDA recommends that empagliflozin be discontinued at least 3 days before scheduled surgery [5]. The hold before this operation was shorter than that recommendation.

Sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitors promote glycosuria, reduce insulin requirements, and increase ketogenesis. Surgical stress, prolonged fasting, and reduced carbohydrate intake are precipitants of perioperative ketoacidosis. The risk of diabetic ketoacidosis has been reported to be increased up to three-fold in patients receiving SGLT2 inhibitors [CITATION NEEDED: source of the three-fold estimate, with population and comparator].

On postoperative day 1, the surgical team attributed her nausea, vomiting, and abdominal discomfort to postoperative ileus and opioid analgesia. Capillary glucose was 182 mg/dL. On the evening of postoperative day 1 she became tachypneic (respiratory rate 28/min). A venous blood gas showed pH 7.12, bicarbonate 9 mmol/L, and an anion gap of 24 mmol/L; lactate was 1.4 mmol/L. Serum beta-hydroxybutyrate was 6.8 mmol/L, and euDKA was diagnosed on the morning of postoperative day 2, approximately 14 hours after the first abnormal gas.

She was transferred to the intensive care unit and treated with intravenous insulin infusion with concurrent 10% dextrose, fluid resuscitation, and potassium replacement. The anion gap closed after 38 hours. Empagliflozin was discontinued permanently, and she was discharged on postoperative day 6 on metformin and basal insulin. We cannot tell from one case whether earlier recognition would have shortened the 38 hours until the anion gap closed, or whether a 3-day preoperative hold would have prevented euDKA.

## Conclusion

The diagnosis of euDKA was made on the morning of postoperative day 2 in a woman who had taken empagliflozin the morning before laparoscopic cholecystectomy. Capillary glucose was 182 mg/dL, and approximately 14 hours elapsed between the first abnormal blood gas and the diagnosis. The preoperative hold was shorter than the 3 days the FDA recommends [5]. In a patient with that history, tachypnea should prompt ketone measurement even at a glucose of 182 mg/dL.
