## Discussion

In this retrospective cohort of 1,146 patients with dementia, mobilization within 24 hours of hip fracture surgery was associated with lower 30-day mortality than delayed mobilization (6.2% vs 9.8%; adjusted odds ratio, 0.61; 95% CI, 0.39–0.95). Length of stay was also shorter with mobilization within 24 hours (median 8 vs 11 days). Delirium incidence did not differ between groups (31% vs 29%).

Patients mobilized within 24 hours may already have been more mobile before surgery than those mobilized later, because mobilization was not randomized. If so, the comparison would be biased toward lower mortality with mobilization within 24 hours, even if that timing had no effect. Preoperative ambulatory status was not recorded for 18% of patients.

A trial that randomizes patients with dementia to mobilization within 24 hours of hip fracture surgery or to delayed mobilization, with 30-day mortality as the primary outcome, would be needed to test whether the association is causal.
