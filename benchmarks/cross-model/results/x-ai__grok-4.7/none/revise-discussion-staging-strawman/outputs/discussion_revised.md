## Discussion

The central question of this study was whether early mobilization after hip fracture surgery is associated with better short-term outcomes in patients with dementia. In this retrospective cohort of 1,146 patients with dementia, mobilization within 24 hours of surgery was associated with lower 30-day mortality (6.2% vs 9.8%; adjusted odds ratio, 0.61; 95% CI, 0.39–0.95) and shorter length of stay (median 8 vs 11 days). These associations are consistent with a possible benefit of earlier mobilization, but they do not establish that early mobilization caused the observed differences. Patients who were mobilized later may have been more medically unstable, more frail, or less able to participate in physiotherapy, and residual confounding by indication remains plausible despite adjustment.

Delayed mobilization should not be interpreted as uniformly harmful. Some patients with severe dementia may be unable to participate in early physiotherapy, and this study does not directly show that early mobilization is feasible or safe for every patient in that subgroup. Delirium incidence did not differ between groups (31% vs 29%). A null difference does not prove that early mobilization has no cognitive effects; the study may have been underpowered to detect a modest difference, and delirium ascertainment may have been incomplete.

Information on preoperative ambulatory status was not recorded for 18% of patients. Missing documentation does not imply that these patients were nonambulatory before the fracture; the missing values could reflect incomplete charting rather than a true inability to walk. Because preoperative mobility is a plausible confounder of both the decision to mobilize early and subsequent outcomes, this missingness limits interpretation of the adjusted associations. No comparison table is presented here. Overall, early mobilization was associated with lower 30-day mortality and shorter length of stay in this cohort, but causality cannot be inferred from these observational data, and the clinical importance of the association should be judged alongside the risk of residual confounding and incomplete baseline mobility data.
