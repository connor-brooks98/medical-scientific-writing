Introduction

Atrial fibrillation (AF) is the most common sustained cardiac arrhythmia and an important cause of ischemic stroke, heart failure, and death. Prevalence rises steeply with age, and adults aged 75 years and older account for a disproportionate share of AF-related morbidity and of eligibility for oral anticoagulation. [CITE] Because AF is often paroxysmal or asymptomatic, it may be missed by symptom-driven assessment or a single clinic electrocardiogram. [CITE] Oral anticoagulation reduces stroke risk in patients with diagnosed AF and elevated thromboembolic risk, but evidence that population screening itself improves clinical outcomes remains limited. [CITE] Strategies that rely on consumer devices therefore require diagnostic accuracy data generated in the populations in which those devices will be used.

Smartwatches that analyze photoplethysmography (PPG), and in some models record a single-lead electrocardiogram (ECG), can identify irregular rhythms outside conventional clinical settings. Studies in younger and mixed-age cohorts have reported high sensitivity and specificity for AF detection. [CITE] These estimates may not generalize to adults aged 75 years and older. Tremor, frequent ectopy, peripheral vascular disease, skin changes, and reduced peripheral perfusion can degrade PPG signal quality and alter classification performance. [CITE] Brief reference ECGs also fail to capture paroxysmal AF. Prolonged ambulatory ECG monitoring is a more appropriate reference standard, yet the accuracy of smartwatch algorithms against multiday patch monitoring has seldom been quantified specifically in the oldest adults. [CITE]

We conducted a prospective diagnostic accuracy study to evaluate a commercially available smartwatch for the detection of AF in adults aged 75 years and older, using 14-day continuous ECG patch monitoring as the reference standard.
