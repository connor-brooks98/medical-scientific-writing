## What already works (keep when you rewrite)

- **P2, S1** uses the right evidence-matched verb ("was associated with") for a retrospective analysis, includes concrete numbers (`2,914 patients`, `adjusted odds ratio, 0.78; 95% CI, 0.64–0.94`), and uses a proper en dash in the numeric range (`0.64–0.94`).
- Paragraph 2's sentence lengths vary naturally, and the draft avoids em dashes and decorative formatting.

## Five revisions that would most improve this Introduction

1. **Remove the causal leap from retrospective data (P2, S2) and align every verb with study design (P1, S3).** Replace "Early norepinephrine therefore prevents death in septic shock" with the limitation of observational data (such as confounding by indication or unmeasured severity) that motivates a prospective study. Verify that any prior work cited in P1 uses observational verbs unless it is an RCT.
2. **Name an explicit gap before introducing your study (between P2, S1 and P2, S3).** Right now the draft claims early norepinephrine improves survival and prevents death, then abruptly invokes "this critical gap" without ever stating what is unknown. State the specific methodological or knowledge gap (e.g., retrospective confounding, unclear time cutoff, or lack of prospective evaluation) and why it matters clinically.
3. **Replace vague, uncited allusions in Paragraph 1 with specific, cited evidence.** Cut the boilerplate opening two sentences (P1, S1–S2) and the redundant fourth sentence (P1, S4), and anchor the background in named prior studies or guidelines with their designs, populations, timing thresholds, and citations.
4. **Clarify the institutional 2,914-patient analysis (P2, S1).** Add its citation (or state that it is unpublished preliminary data from your centre), specify the comparator group (e.g., norepinephrine started after 1 hour), and confirm it is prior work rather than a result of the current study.
5. **Rewrite the closing sentence (P2, S3) into a concrete objective statement.** Drop the "not only ... but also" structure, "fill an important gap", and "pave the way for improved outcomes". State what your study tests, its exact prospective design, the patient population, the intervention and comparator, and the primary outcome.
