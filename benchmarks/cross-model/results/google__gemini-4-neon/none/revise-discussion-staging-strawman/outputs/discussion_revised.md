## Discussion

In this retrospective cohort of 1,146 patients with dementia undergoing hip fracture surgery, mobilization within 24 hours of surgery was associated with lower 30-day mortality (6.2% vs 9.8%; adjusted odds ratio, 0.61; 95% CI, 0.39–0.95) and a shorter length of stay (median, 8 vs 11 days). Although clinicians may hesitate to initiate early physiotherapy in patients with cognitive impairment out of concern for safety or limited participation, these results do not suggest excess short-term harm and support further evaluation of early mobilization in this population.

Delirium incidence did not differ between groups (31% vs 29%). Although early mobilization neither reduced nor increased observed delirium rates, this null result does not exclude cognitive effects. Modest differences in delirium or effects on other cognitive domains may have gone undetected given the retrospective design and sample size.

Several limitations warrant consideration. Because this was a retrospective observational study, causality cannot be inferred, and the observed associations with lower mortality and shorter hospitalization may partly reflect confounding by indication: patients mobilized within 24 hours may have been healthier, more medically stable, or less severely impaired at baseline than those whose mobilization was delayed. In addition, preoperative ambulatory status was not recorded for 18% of patients. Because missing documentation cannot be assumed to indicate nonambulatory status, incomplete baseline mobility data may have introduced residual confounding, as prefracture ambulatory function strongly influences both postoperative mobilization and survival. Moreover, whether these associations extend equally to patients with severe dementia warrants dedicated evaluation.

In conclusion, among patients with dementia undergoing hip fracture surgery, mobilization within 24 hours was associated with lower 30-day mortality and shorter length of stay, with no observed difference in delirium. Prospective studies with complete baseline functional data and stratified cognitive assessments are needed to determine whether early mobilization improves outcomes across the spectrum of dementia severity.
