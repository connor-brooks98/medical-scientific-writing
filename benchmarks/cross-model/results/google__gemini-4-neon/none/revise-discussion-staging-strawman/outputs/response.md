### What was reading off in the original

**Methodological and logical issues**
- **Missing-data fallacy:** The original stated that because preoperative ambulatory status was unrecorded for 18% of patients, those patients were "likely nonambulatory." Missing chart documentation cannot be assumed to reflect nonambulatory status; instead, it is a limitation that could cause residual confounding (since prefracture mobility affects both early mobilization and survival).
- **Causal overreach / missing confounding discussion:** Phrases like *"illustrates the importance"* and *"appears to be beneficial"* lean toward causality without acknowledging confounding by indication (patients mobilized within 24 hours may have been more medically stable or less frail at baseline).
- **Unsupported claims:**
  - Claiming your findings refute concerns about *severe* dementia overreaches unless you have stratified data by dementia severity.
  - Claiming early mobilization was *"feasible for most patients"* is unsupported without citing the actual proportion of the cohort mobilized within 24 hours.
- **Dangling reference:** *"The table below compares our findings with previous cohorts"* points to a table that is not present.

**Tone and prose issues**
- Several passages read like rhetorical filler or essay prose rather than clinical reporting (*"At its core, the central question..."*, *"Mobility is the currency of recovery..."*, *"This is not to say..."*, *"One might argue..."*, *"A lack of difference... does not mean X. It means Y."*, *"The key point is..."*).
