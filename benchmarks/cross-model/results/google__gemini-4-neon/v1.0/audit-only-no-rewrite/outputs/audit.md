# Audit of `introduction.txt`

*Reading assumed: Introduction for a prospective clinical study manuscript (or study protocol, given the future tense in the final sentence).*

**What works:** Paragraph 2, Sentence 1 uses an evidence-matched verb ("was associated with") for a retrospective study and reports an adjusted effect estimate with a 95% CI. Once its citation or preliminary-data status is clarified, that sentence structure is worth keeping.

## Five revisions that would most improve this piece

1. **Remove the causal overclaim from retrospective data and calibrate prior claims to study design (P2, S2; P1, S3).** Delete "Early norepinephrine therefore prevents death in septic shock," which overstates an observational odds ratio and undercuts the rationale for a new study. In P1, S3, use "improves" only if you cite randomized trial evidence for survival; otherwise switch to observational wording.
2. **Define the actual gap (between P2, S1 and P2, S3).** Before saying you are addressing a gap, state specifically what existing evidence—including your institutional cohort—leaves unresolved (for example, confounding by indication, immortal time bias, lack of prospective timing capture, or lack of randomized evidence).
3. **Replace vague allusions with named, cited specifics and clarify the provenance of the institutional cohort (P1, S3–S4; P2, S1).** Replace "Studies have shown" and "a growing body of evidence" with cited studies, their designs, populations, and outcomes; drop P1, S4 if it merely repeats S3 in reverse; and either cite the 2,914-patient institutional analysis or identify it as unpublished preliminary data.
4. **State the concrete aim and design in the closing sentence (P2, S3).** Replace "a comprehensive prospective study" and the "pave the way" coda with your specific study design, target population, vasopressor-timing exposure or intervention, comparator, and primary outcome, using past tense if the study is already completed.
5. **Strip the generic opening and sentence-level AI tells (P1, S1–S2, S4).** Cut the global-burden opener, "growing interest" filler, `-ing` tail, stacked hedges ("suggests ... may potentially be associated with"), and inflated vocabulary ("pivotal", "evolving landscape", "comprehensive", "pave the way"), and standardize on one term for the exposure.
