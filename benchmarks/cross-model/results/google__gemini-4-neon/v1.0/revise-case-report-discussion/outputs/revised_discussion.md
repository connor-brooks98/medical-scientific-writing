## Discussion

Our patient developed euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis (euDKA) after an uncomplicated elective laparoscopic cholecystectomy. Although the FDA recommends stopping empagliflozin at least 3 days before scheduled surgery [5], she was instructed to hold metformin on the morning of surgery while empagliflozin was continued until the morning before the operation, and she fasted for 14 hours. Sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitors promote glycosuria, reduce insulin requirements, and enhance ketogenesis. Prior studies have reported up to a three-fold higher risk of DKA in patients receiving SGLT2 inhibitors [CITATION NEEDED: named study, design, population, comparator, and risk estimate]. Combined with surgical stress, prolonged fasting, and reduced carbohydrate intake, continuing empagliflozin likely precipitated her ketoacidosis.

The diagnosis was delayed at two points. On POD 1, nausea, vomiting, and abdominal discomfort were attributed to postoperative ileus and opioid analgesia in the absence of marked hyperglycemia (capillary glucose 182 mg/dL). That evening she became tachypneic (respiratory rate 28/min), and a venous blood gas showed high-anion-gap metabolic acidosis (pH 7.12, bicarbonate 9 mmol/L, anion gap 24 mmol/L) with a lactate of 1.4 mmol/L. Even then, euDKA was not diagnosed until the morning of POD 2, approximately 14 hours after the first abnormal gas, when serum beta-hydroxybutyrate was 6.8 mmol/L.

After transfer to the intensive care unit, her anion gap closed in 38 hours on intravenous insulin with 10% dextrose, fluid resuscitation, and potassium replacement. Empagliflozin was stopped permanently, and she was discharged on POD 6. From a single case, we cannot determine whether earlier ketone testing would have prevented intensive care admission or shortened the time to anion-gap closure.

## Conclusion

In this patient, continuing empagliflozin until the day before elective laparoscopic cholecystectomy preceded postoperative euDKA that remained undiagnosed for approximately 14 hours after metabolic acidosis appeared on venous blood gas. Preoperative medication instructions should direct patients to stop empagliflozin at least 3 days before scheduled surgery, as recommended by the FDA. Postoperatively, nausea, vomiting, abdominal discomfort, or high-anion-gap metabolic acidosis in a patient taking an SGLT2 inhibitor warrants serum ketone measurement regardless of capillary glucose.
