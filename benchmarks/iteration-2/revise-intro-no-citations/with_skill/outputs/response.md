**Questions for you**

- **Gap claim:** "Few of these studies enrolled adults aged 75 years and older" is a sharper claim than your "remains poorly understood". Check that the literature supports it, ideally with the median age or the proportion aged 75 and older in the main cohorts. If it doesn't hold, go back to "is uncertain".
- **Stroke claim:** Your draft said early detection "may lead to improved outcomes, including reduced stroke risk". As far as I recall, randomized screening trials have reported small or non-significant reductions in stroke. If your references confirm that, the sentence should read "Whether detecting AF by screening reduces stroke is uncertain". A reviewer is likely to raise this point.
- **Index test:** "Diagnostic accuracy" doesn't say which output you assessed: the PPG irregular-rhythm notification, the ECG app classification, or both. STARD asks the Introduction to give the intended use and clinical role of the index test (for example, opportunistic screening or triage before confirmatory ECG). One sentence on this would close that reporting item. I didn't write it because I don't know your design.
- **Mechanism sentence:** Please confirm it matches how you frame the problem. It is standard physiology, so I didn't tag it for a citation.
