## Main changes

1. **The stroke-benefit claim is more cautious.** The draft said early detection "may lead to improved outcomes, including reduced stroke risk." Reviewers in this area know that LOOP and STROKESTOP gave modest or null results, and that NOAH-AFNET 6 and ARTESiA left the value of treating short device-detected AF unclear. The revision states that uncertainty directly. That actually helps your case: if the benefit of screening is uncertain, the accuracy of the screening tool and its false-positive rate matter more.
2. **The gap is now argued, not just asserted.** "Remains poorly understood" became a specific argument with three parts. The earlier validation studies enrolled mostly younger people. There are concrete ways that age could lower accuracy (tremor, PVD, ectopy, dexterity). And false positives cost more in people over 75 because of follow-up testing, anxiety and bleeding risk on anticoagulation.
3. **I added the guideline context.** The ESC 2024 and USPSTF positions differ, and that split is one reason for testing in the 75+ group specifically.
4. **I cut the promotional language.** "Major global health burden… millions," "transform the landscape," "critical gap," "comprehensive" and the whole closing sentence ("fills an important gap… pave the way…") are gone. Most journals and reviewers object to that kind of wording, and claims about what the study contributes belong in the Discussion.
5. **I sharpened the aim.** The last paragraph now names the design (prospective diagnostic accuracy study), the population, the index test, the reference standard and the primary objective, which is what STARD expects in an Introduction.
6. **Smaller edits:** "Studies have shown" now names the evidence base it refers to. "Single-lead ECG" and "PPG" are now separate features. The em-dash aside is gone, and the sentences are shorter.

## Assumptions and things to check

- **Index test.** I assumed the device uses PPG notifications, single-lead ECG or both, and kept the aim sentence general ("the smartwatch"). If you evaluated only one feature, say so in the aim, e.g. "the irregular rhythm notification feature."
- **Primary objective.** I assumed it is sensitivity and specificity. If the protocol names other primary or secondary outcomes (PPV/NPV, proportion of unclassifiable recordings, per-participant vs per-episode analysis), adjust the last sentence. Reporting the unclassifiable rate would fit well, since the Introduction now raises it.
- **Reference standard.** I kept "14-day continuous ECG patch monitoring." If the watch and patch were worn at the same time, add "concurrent." It makes the design clearer and helps with STARD item 10.
- **Device name.** I left it as "a commercially available smartwatch." Some journals want the make and model in the Introduction; most are fine with it appearing only in Methods.
- **Mechanisms (R11).** The tremor, PVD, ectopy and dexterity points are plausible mechanisms, written as "may." If you can't find support for one of them, cut it rather than leave it unreferenced.
