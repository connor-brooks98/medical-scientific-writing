## Discussion

Euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis (euDKA) is an uncommon but potentially life-threatening adverse effect of sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitors, which are now used in the treatment of type 2 diabetes, heart failure, and chronic kidney disease. By promoting glycosuria, these agents lower insulin requirements and enhance ketogenesis [ref]. The risk of DKA is up to three times higher in patients taking SGLT2 inhibitors [ref]. Surgical stress, prolonged fasting, and reduced carbohydrate intake are recognized precipitants [ref], which places surgical patients on these drugs at particular risk. Our patient had a 14-hour preoperative fast, underwent abdominal surgery, and developed nausea and vomiting on POD 1.

Because glucose is often only modestly elevated in euDKA, the diagnosis can be delayed. In our patient, nausea, vomiting, and abdominal discomfort on POD 1 were attributed to postoperative ileus and opioid analgesia, both common after abdominal surgery, and a capillary glucose of 182 mg/dL did not raise concern for DKA. Her tachypnea that evening was, in retrospect, consistent with respiratory compensation for a severe metabolic acidosis. The venous blood gas showed a pH of 7.12, a bicarbonate of 9 mmol/L, and an anion gap of 24 mmol/L. A normal lactate (1.4 mmol/L) made lactic acidosis, a relevant alternative in a patient taking metformin, unlikely, and a beta-hydroxybutyrate of 6.8 mmol/L confirmed ketoacidosis. The diagnosis was nonetheless made approximately 14 hours after the first abnormal gas. Measuring ketones in any patient taking an SGLT2 inhibitor who presents with nausea, vomiting, or malaise could have shortened this interval.

The FDA recommends that empagliflozin be discontinued at least 3 days before scheduled surgery [5]. Our patient was instructed to hold metformin on the morning of surgery, but empagliflozin was continued, and her last dose was taken on the morning before surgery. This shortened interval, together with her prolonged fast, likely contributed to the development of euDKA.

She was treated with an intravenous insulin infusion with concurrent 10% dextrose, fluid resuscitation, and potassium replacement, and the anion gap closed after 38 hours. Empagliflozin was discontinued permanently, and she was discharged on POD 6 on metformin and basal insulin.

## Conclusion

In postoperative patients taking SGLT2 inhibitors, euDKA should be considered when nausea, vomiting, abdominal discomfort, or tachypnea develop, even if glucose is only modestly elevated. In our patient, these findings were initially attributed to ileus and opioids, and the diagnosis was made approximately 14 hours after the first abnormal blood gas. Holding SGLT2 inhibitors for at least 3 days before elective surgery, as the FDA recommends for empagliflozin [5], and checking ketones early in symptomatic patients may help prevent this complication or shorten the time to diagnosis.
