## Discussion

Our patient developed euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis (euDKA) on POD 1 after laparoscopic cholecystectomy while taking empagliflozin, a sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitor. SGLT2 inhibitors are prescribed for type 2 diabetes, heart failure, and chronic kidney disease. They promote glycosuria, reduce insulin requirements, and enhance ketogenesis [CITATION NEEDED: mechanism]. Because glucose continues to be lost in the urine, ketoacidosis can develop without the hyperglycemia that usually prompts testing for DKA. DKA is uncommon in patients taking these drugs [CITATION NEEDED: incidence], but the risk has been reported to be up to three-fold higher [CITATION NEEDED: source and comparator group for the three-fold estimate].

The diagnosis in our patient was delayed. Her nausea, vomiting, and abdominal discomfort on POD 1 were attributed to postoperative ileus and opioid analgesia, and her capillary glucose of 182 mg/dL did not point toward DKA. When she became tachypneic that evening, a venous blood gas showed a high anion gap metabolic acidosis (pH 7.12, anion gap 24 mmol/L). Lactate was 1.4 mmol/L, which made lactic acidosis unlikely in a patient taking metformin, and beta-hydroxybutyrate was 6.8 mmol/L. The diagnosis of euDKA was made on the morning of POD 2, approximately 14 hours after the first abnormal gas. Delayed recognition when glucose is not markedly raised has been described in earlier reports of SGLT2 inhibitor-associated euDKA [CITATION NEEDED: case series describing delayed recognition; ideally also perioperative cases with time to diagnosis].

Several recognized precipitants were present at once. The FDA recommends that empagliflozin be discontinued at least 3 days before scheduled surgery [5]. Our patient was told to hold metformin on the morning of surgery, but empagliflozin was continued, and her last dose was taken the morning before surgery. She had also fasted for 14 hours before the operation and was vomiting on POD 1. Surgical stress, prolonged fasting, and reduced carbohydrate intake each predispose patients taking SGLT2 inhibitors to ketoacidosis [CITATION NEEDED: source for these precipitants]. Continuing empagliflozin until the day before surgery likely contributed to her euDKA. From a single case, however, we cannot separate the effect of the drug from that of the fast and the operation, or say whether a 3-day hold would have prevented the episode.

The acidosis resolved with insulin infusion and concurrent 10% dextrose, and the anion gap closed after 38 hours. We do not know whether making the diagnosis at the time of the first abnormal gas would have shortened her stay in the intensive care unit.

## Conclusion

Our patient developed euDKA on POD 1 after continuing empagliflozin until the day before surgery, and the diagnosis was made approximately 14 hours after the first abnormal blood gas. Her capillary glucose of 182 mg/dL did not exclude DKA. In patients taking an SGLT2 inhibitor, preoperative instructions should address the drug explicitly (for empagliflozin, discontinuation at least 3 days before surgery [5]), and postoperative nausea, vomiting, or tachypnea should prompt a blood gas and ketone measurement regardless of the glucose level.
